\section{Well-definedness}
\label{sec:b-wd}

Many B operators are \emph{partial}: they yield a meaningful value only on a strict subset
of their well-typed arguments. Function application \(\appB' f\,x\) presupposes that \(f\)
denotes a function and that \(x\) lies in its domain; cardinality \(\cardB{S}\) presupposes
that \(S\) is finite; the extrema \(\minB(S)\) and \(\maxB(S)\) presuppose that \(S\) is
finite and nonempty. Typing (\Cref{sec:b-typing}) constrains the \emph{shape} of these
expressions, but cannot express such conditions: they depend on the \emph{values} the
subterms denote, not on their syntax. The missing layer is \emph{well-definedness}---the
side conditions under which every partial operator in a term is applied within its domain.

\paragraph{Well-definedness in the B method.}
Partiality is a first-class concern of the proof-obligation generator. For each partial
operator occurring in a proof obligation, Atelier~B emits a separate \emph{well-definedness}
(WD) obligation---a side goal asserting that the operator is used within its domain---and
assumes that condition when discharging the main goal~\cite{abrial1996bbook}
(see~\Cref{sec:b-method}). A partial operator is read as a bounded Hilbert choice
\(\epsilon\,x \inB S \cdot \varphi\), left unspecified when no element of \(S\) satisfies
\(\varphi\); its WD condition is precisely that some element does. This keeps the goal logic
total, at the price of detaching the conditions from the operators that induce them: the
obligations are generated globally, and their correspondence with the partial operators of
the goal is left implicit.

\paragraph{A semantic account.}
The denotational semantics of \Cref{sec:b-semantics} instead takes partiality at face value:
the denotation \(\interpB{\cdot}\) is a genuine \emph{partial} function on terms, undefined
exactly when a partial operator is evaluated outside its domain. Well-definedness is then not
an external catalogue of obligations but a single predicate \(\regnotation{\WD}\) that
collects, by structural recursion on a term, the conditions under which all of its partial
operators denote. Each condition is stated directly against the denotations of subterms---the
finiteness of a denoted set, the functionality of a denoted relation---so that
well-definedness is grounded in the very semantic objects it constrains. We introduce the
predicate ahead of the denotation because it is exactly the precondition under which that
denotation is total (\Cref{thm:b-denote-complete}).

\begin{definition}[Well-definedness predicate][B.PHOAS.WellDefined][B/Reasoning/WellDefined.html\#B.PHOAS.WellDefined]
  \label{def:b-welldefined}
  The predicate \(\WD : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\BDom \to \mathsf{Prop}\) is defined by structural
  recursion. On variables, literals, and the type constants, and on every total constructor, it
  is purely propagational:
  \[
    \WD(t) \triangleq
    \begin{cases}
      \top
        & t \text{ a variable, an integer or Boolean literal, or } \ZintB,\ \ZboolB; \\[2pt]
      \WD(t_1) \land \cdots \land \WD(t_k)
        & t = c(t_1, \dots, t_k),\ c \text{ neither a binder nor a partial operator}; \\[2pt]
      \WD(D) \land \forall \vec v,\ \WD\big(P(\vec v)\big)
        & t \text{ a binder } \{D \mid P\},\ \lambdaB' D \cdot P, \text{ or } \forallB' D \cdot P,
    \end{cases}
  \]
  where the binder clause requires the body \(P\) to be well-defined under every instantiation
  \(\vec v\) of its bound variables. At the four partial operators, \(\WD\) requires the
  well-definedness of the immediate subterms together with the semantic side condition that
  makes the operator denote:
  \begin{description}
    \item[\(\appB' f\,x\):] writing \(\interpB{f} = \denval{F}[\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)]\) and
          \(\interpB{x} = \denval{X}[\tau]\), the value \(F\) is a partial function from
          \(\interpZ{\tau}\) to \(\interpZ{\sigma}\) and \(X\) belongs to its domain:
          \(\IsPFunc\ F\ \interpZ{\tau}\ \interpZ{\sigma}\) and \(X \in \Dom(F)\);
    \item[\(\cardB{S}\):] writing \(\interpB{S} = \denval{S'}[\setB\alpha]\), the value \(S'\)
          is finite: \(\IsFinite\ S'\);
    \item[\(\minB(S),\ \maxB(S)\):] writing \(\interpB{S} = \denval{S'}[\setB\alpha]\), the value
          \(S'\) is finite and nonempty: \(\IsFinite\ S'\) and \(S' \neq \emptyset\).
  \end{description}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Role.}
The predicate is vacuous on the total fragment and contributes one condition at each occurrence
of a partial operator; in particular, \(\WD(t)\) holds trivially whenever \(t\) is built without
\(\appB'\), \(\cardB{\cdot}\), \(\minB\), or \(\maxB\). Its significance is fixed by the
typing-completeness theorem (\Cref{thm:b-denote-complete}): a well-typed term denotes a value of
its static type \emph{as soon as it is well-defined}. Indeed, \(\WD(t)\) supplies exactly the
witnesses---functionality and domain membership for application, finiteness and nonemptiness for
cardinality and the extrema---that the partial clauses of \(\interpB{\cdot}\)
(\Cref{subsec:b-denote}) require in order to select their defined branch. Well-definedness is
thus the precise boundary between the partial denotation and the total restriction on which the
certified encoding of \Cref{ch:beer} relies. Defined here on the abstract syntax used for
semantic reasoning (\Cref{sec:b-syntax}), the predicate transfers to the concrete first-order
syntax through abstraction, so that obligations stated over concrete terms inherit the same
discipline.

\begin{example}[Propagation and side conditions]
  The term \(\minB(S) \leB \maxB(S)\), with \(S\) a free variable of type \(\setB\intB\), has
  \(\minB\) and \(\maxB\) as its only partial operators. Unfolding \Cref{def:b-welldefined}, its
  well-definedness reduces to the single requirement that \(S\) denote a finite, nonempty set,
  the comparison \(\leB\) and the variable \(S\) contributing nothing. The companion term
  \(\cardB{S}\) is well-defined under the weaker requirement that \(S\) merely denote a finite
  set.
\end{example}
