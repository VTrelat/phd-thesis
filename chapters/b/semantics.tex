\section{Semantics}
\label{sec:b-semantics}

The semantics of B developed in this work is \emph{denotational}:
each term is assigned a value in a set-theoretic domain using the ZFLean framework
developed in \Cref{ch:zflean}.
Denotational semantics provides the compositional mathematical objects required
to assign meanings to B terms, which is the central concern of the following chapters;
the emphasis is on \emph{what} a B expression computes, not on \emph{how} it is
evaluated---compared to, say, an operational semantics.
Concrete terms are interpreted after abstraction to PHOAS, as described in \Cref{sec:b-syntax}.

\subsection{Interpretation of B types}
\label{subsec:b-type-interp}

We first define the interpretation of B types as ZFC sets, denoted \(\interpZ{\cdot}\).

\begin{definition}[Type interpretation][B.BType.toZFSet][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.BType.toZFSet]
\label{def:b-type-interp}
The function $\interpZ{\cdot} : \mathsf{BType} \to \mathsf{ZFSet}$ is defined by:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\interpZ{\intB} &=& \Zint,\\
\interpZ{\boolB} &=& \Zbool,\\
\interpZ{\setB\tau} &=& \powZ(\interpZ{\tau}),\\
\interpZ{\tau \prodB \sigma} &=& \interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}.
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

Agreeing on the fact that this set-theoretic interpretation is appropriate, we can show
that B types are inhabited.

\begin{lemma}[Nonemptiness][B.BType.toZFSet\_nonempty][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.BType.toZFSet_nonempty]
\label{thm:b-type-nonempty}
Interpretations of B types are nonempty, \ie
\[\forall \tau,\, \interpZ{\tau} \neq \emptyset\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By structural induction on \(\tau\).
The sets \(\Zint\) and \(\Zbool\) are nonempty by construction;
\(\emptyset \in \powZ(S)\) for any \(S\);
the Cartesian product of two nonempty sets is nonempty.
\end{proof}

Nonemptiness ensures that open terms can be given canonical default values, and
that the semantic domain is inhabited at every type.

\subsection{Semantic domain}
\label{subsec:b-sem-domain}

We now define the semantic domain of B terms, \ie the type of interpretations of B terms.
As mentioned already, the B mathematical language is rooted in set theory, so the semantic domain
is naturally expressed over set-theoretic constructions.
Our interpretation of B terms slightly diverges from the standard description given in the
B-Book~\cite{abrial1996bbook} in that we constrain the semantic domain to carry intrinsic B types,
as motivated earlier in \Cref{rem:b-constraint-on-V}, so that we achieve that denotations are only
defined for \emph{well-typed} values with respect to our type system.
The reason for this design choice is to avoid set-theoretic pathologies related to ill-typed terms,
and provide baked-in typing constraints directly in the semantics, thus circumventing some
well-definedness side conditions---actually, all conditions related to type-correctness---in the
denotation clauses.
This is highly motivated by the idea of obtaining a \emph{correct-by-construction} design.

\begin{remark}
  This design choice only removes case analysis on type-correctness from the denotation clauses, but does not eliminate the need for semantic well-definedness side conditions related to partial operations, like in domain-application or cardinality, which are still present in the semantics and are discussed below.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Semantic domain][B.Dom][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.Dom]
\label{def:b-dom}
The semantic domain is the following dependent sum type:
\[
  \mathsf{Dom} \triangleq
  \sum_{X : \mathsf{ZFSet}}\sum_{\tau : \mathsf{BType}}\,
  X \in \interpZ{\tau}
\]
\end{definition}

Elements in \(\mathsf{Dom}\) are therefore dependent triples, denoted \(\denval{X}[\tau]\),
containing a ZFC set \(X\), a B type \(\tau\) and a proof \(\pf{\tau}{X} : X \in \interpZ{\tau}\).

\begin{note}
  When the proof is unused or irrelevant in a given context, we simply write ``\(\irrelprf\)'' in
  such triples, mimicking Lean notations.
\end{note}

As motivated in \Cref{subsec:phoas-for-semantics}, we now use PHOAS representation as a trick to define the semantics of B.
The denotation function is therefore a partial function---due to B containing partial constructs requiring well-definedness side conditions---over PHOAS terms parameterized by the semantic domain:
\[
  \interpB{\cdot} :
  \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\mathsf{Dom}
  \rightharpoonup \mathsf{Option}\,\mathsf{Dom}
\]
The immediate consequence of this device is that variables denote themselves.

\subsection{Denotation}
\label{subsec:b-denote}

The denotation function is defined by structural recursion over PHOAS terms, as described in the following definition.
In the clauses below, \(\throw\) is used when denotation cannot be defined due to a failure of a well-definedness side condition.

\begin{definition}[Denotation][B.denote][B/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#B.denote]
\label{def:b-denote}

\paragraph{Literals and constants.}
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\interpB{v}               &=& v                    & (v : \mathsf{Dom}),\\[2pt]
\interpB{n}               &=& \overline{n} : \intB  & (n \in \mathbb{Z}),\\[2pt]
\interpB{b}               &=& \overline{b} : \boolB & (b \in \mathbb{B}),\\[2pt]
\interpB{\ZintB}          &=& \Zint   : \setB\intB, \\[2pt]
\interpB{\ZboolB}         &=& \Zbool  : \setB\boolB,
\end{array}
\]
where $\overline{n}$ and $\overline{b}$ are the ZFC encodings of integers and
Booleans (Chapter~\ref{ch:zflean}), $\ZintB$ and $\ZboolB$ are the B universe
constants for integers and Booleans.

\paragraph{Pair and arithmetic.}
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\interpB{t \mapletB u} &=& \pairZ{X}{Y} : \tau \prodB \sigma
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \tau,\; \interpB{u} = Y : \sigma,\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \addB u}    &=& X \addZ Y : \intB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \intB,\; \interpB{u} = Y : \intB;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \subB u}    &=& X \subZ Y : \intB & \text{(idem)},\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \mulB u}    &=& X \mulZ Y : \intB & \text{(idem)}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Comparisons and Boolean operations.}
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\interpB{t \leB u}   &=& X \leqZ Y   : \boolB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \intB,\; \interpB{u} = Y : \intB;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{\notB t}    &=& \negZ X : \boolB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \boolB;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \andB u}  &=& X \andZ Y : \boolB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \boolB,\; \interpB{u} = Y : \boolB;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \eqB u}   &=& X \eqZ Y  : \boolB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \tau,\; \interpB{u} = Y : \tau \text{ for some } \tau;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{t \inB S}   &=& (X \in S') : \boolB
  & \text{if } \interpB{t} = X : \tau,\; \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\tau;\;\throw\text{ otherwise}.
\end{array}
\]
Equality and membership require both operands to carry the same B-type tag.

\paragraph{Set-theoretic constructors.}
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\interpB{S \cupB T} &=& S' \cupZ T' : \setB\tau
  & \text{if } \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\tau,\; \interpB{T} = T' : \setB\tau;\;\throw\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{S \capB T} &=& S' \capZ T' : \setB\tau & \text{(idem)},\\[2pt]
\interpB{S \prodB T} &=& S' \timesZ T' : \setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)
  & \text{if } \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\tau,\; \interpB{T} = T' : \setB\sigma,\\[2pt]
\interpB{\powB S}   &=& \powZ(S') : \setB(\setB\tau)
  & \text{if } \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\tau.
\end{array}
\]
Union and intersection require a common element type; failing this, the
denotation is $\bot$.

\paragraph{Partial function type.}

Partial function types are interpreted as sets of relation graphs:
\[
  \interpB{A \pfunB B}
  =
  \bigl\{ f \in \powZ(A' \timesZ B') \bigm| f \text{ is a partial function} \bigr\}
  : \setB\bigl(\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)\bigr),
\]
where $\interpB{A} = A' : \setB\tau$ and $\interpB{B} = B' : \setB\sigma$.

\paragraph{Partial operations.}

Function application evaluates by graph lookup:
\[
  \interpB{f(x)} = y : \sigma
  \quad\text{when}\quad
  \interpB{f} = F : \setB(\tau \prodB \sigma),\;
  \interpB{x} = X : \tau,\;
  F\text{ is a partial function, and }X \in \mathrm{dom}(F),
\]
where $y$ is the unique value such that $\pairZ{X}{y} \in F$.
If $F$ is not a partial function, or $X \notin \mathrm{dom}(F)$, the
denotation is $\bot$.

Cardinality, minimum, and maximum are defined under finiteness conditions:
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
\interpB{\cardB{S}} &=& \cardZ{S'} : \intB
  & \text{if } \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\tau \text{ and } S' \text{ is finite};\;\bot\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{\minB(S)} &=& \min(S') : \intB
  & \text{if } \interpB{S} = S' : \setB\intB,\; S' \text{ finite and nonempty};\;\bot\text{ otherwise},\\[2pt]
\interpB{\maxB(S)} &=& \maxZ(S') : \intB & \text{(idem)}.
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Binders.}

Binder constructs use the PHOAS representation: bound variables are Lean
functions, so substitution is meta-level function application.

For set comprehension, the domain $D$ denotes a set of tuples of type $\tau$.
The body predicate is evaluated at each tuple, and the denotation is the subset
for which the predicate holds:
\[
  \interpB{\{\vec x \inB D \mid P\}}
  =
  \{ z \in \interpB{D} \mid \interpB{P[z/\vec x]} \eqZ \topZ \} : \setB\tau.
\]

For lambda abstraction, the result is the graph of the function described by
the body:
\[
  \interpB{\lambdaB \vec x \inB D.\,E}
  =
  \bigl\{\pairZ{z}{y} \bigm| z \in \interpB{D}
      \text{ and }
      \interpB{E[z/\vec x]} \eqZ y\bigr\} : \setB(\tau \prodB \gamma),
\]
where $\gamma$ is the return type of $E$, inferred from a representative element
of $\interpB{D}$.

Universal quantification is interpreted as the bounded infimum of the Boolean
values obtained over the domain, taken in the two-element ZFC Boolean
algebra $\Zbool$:
\[
  \interpB{\forallB \vec x \inB D.\,P}
  =
  \bigwedgeZ_{z \in \interpB{D}} \interpB{P[z/\vec x]}.
\]
The empty infimum is $\topZ$ (vacuous truth), consistent with the standard
semantics of universal quantification.
In Lean, this is computed as
$\mathsf{sInter}\bigl(\{\interpB{P[z/\vec x]} \mid z \in \interpB{D}\}
\cap \Zbool\bigr)$, returning $\topZ$ when the domain is empty.

\paragraph{Type-compatibility conditions.}

Several denotation clauses require subterms to carry a common B-type tag:
this is checked on the $\mathsf{BType}$ component of the $\mathsf{Dom}$ elements
returned by the recursive calls.
For equality, union, intersection, set membership, and function application,
the denotation is $\bot$ if this check fails.
When a term is accompanied by a typing derivation, these checks always succeed,
as guaranteed by the semantic soundness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:b-denote-sound} below).

\end{definition}

\subsection{Basic properties}
\label{subsec:b-sem-properties}

The two fundamental properties of the denotation are type determinacy and
soundness.

\begin{theorem}[Type determinacy, \leancodeptr{denote\_welltyped\_eq}{}]
\label{thm:b-denote-type-det}
If $t$ is a well-typed PHOAS term and $\interpB{t} = \denval{V}[\tau]$, then
$\tau$ equals the B type assigned to $t$ by its typing derivation.
\end{theorem}

In other words, if a term has a denotation, its B-type tag is uniquely
determined by the static type: a term of type $\tau$ cannot denote a value
of type $\sigma \neq \tau$.

The soundness theorem states that well-typed terms, under a suitable environment,
always have a denotation of the correct type, provided the partial operations
they contain are well-defined.

\begin{theorem}[Denotation soundness, \leancodeptr{denote\_exists\_of\_typing}{}]
\label{thm:b-denote-sound}
Let $\btype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}$ be a concrete B typing derivation, let
$\Delta : \mathsf{B}.\mathcal{V} \to \mathsf{Option}\,\mathsf{Dom}$ be a
renaming covering all free variables of $t$, and let $t^*$ denote the
abstracted PHOAS term.
If $\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}$ is a well-typed context and $t^*$ is
well-defined, then
\[
  \exists\, V \in \interpB{\tau},\quad
  \interpB{t^*} = \denval{V}[\tau].
\]
\end{theorem}

The \emph{well-definedness} predicate \leancodeptr{WellDefined}{} formalises the
semantic side conditions for partial operations:
for each subterm of the form $f(x)$, it requires $\interpB{f}$ to be a partial
function and $\interpB{x} \in \mathrm{dom}(\interpB{f})$;
for each subterm $\cardB{S}$, $\minB(S)$, or $\maxB(S)$, it requires
$\interpB{S}$ to be finite (and nonempty for $\minB$ and $\maxB$).

The syntax, typing, and semantics described in this chapter form the
source-language contract used by the SMT translation and by the correctness
arguments in the following chapters.
