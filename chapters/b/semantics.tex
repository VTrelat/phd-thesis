\section{Semantics}
\label{sec:b-semantics}

Introduced by Scott and Strachey~\cite{scottStrachey1971}, \emph{denotational semantics} assigns to
every syntactic construct a mathematical object---its \emph{denotation}---in a \emph{compositional}
manner: the meaning of a compound term is determined solely by the meanings of its sub-terms.
Scott's domain theory underpins the framework, providing the mathematical structure needed to
interpret recursive definitions as least fixed points of continuous operators.
Stoy's textbook~\cite{stoy1977} gives a thorough and remarkably accessible account of this
approach; the semantics developed here is denotational and draws inspiration from this book,
though it follows the B-Book~\cite{abrial1996bbook} closely in its treatment of B constructs.

For the expression and predicate fragment, the B-Book's semantics is essentially denotational
in character: B types denote sets, expressions of a given type denote elements of the
corresponding set, and predicates denote truth values, all defined compositionally.
The main departure concerns \emph{substitutions}, whose meanings are given as predicate
transformers rather than direct functions on state.
The emphasis throughout our development is on \emph{what} a B expression computes, not \emph{how} it is
evaluated---in contrast to an operational semantics~\cite{plotkin2004sos}---and concrete
terms are interpreted, after abstraction to PHOAS (as described in \Cref{sec:b-syntax}) over the ZFLean framework of \Cref{ch:zflean}.

\subsection{Interpretation of B types}
\label{subsec:b-type-interp}

We first define the interpretation of B types as ZFC sets, denoted \(\regnotation{\interpZ{\cdot}}\).

\begin{definition}[Type interpretation][B.BType.toZFSet][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.BType.toZFSet]
  \label{def:b-type-interp}
          The function $\interpZ{\cdot} : \mathsf{BType} \to \mathsf{ZFSet}$ is defined by:
  \[
    \begin{array}{rcl}
      \interpZ{\intB}              & = & \Zint,                                   \\
      \interpZ{\boolB}             & = & \Zbool,                                  \\
      \interpZ{\setB\tau}          & = & \powZ(\interpZ{\tau}),                   \\
      \interpZ{\tau \prodB \sigma} & = & \interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}.
    \end{array}
  \]
\end{definition}

Agreeing on the fact that this set-theoretic interpretation is appropriate, we can show
that B types are inhabited.

\begin{lemma}[Nonemptiness][B.BType.toZFSet\_nonempty][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.BType.toZFSet_nonempty]
  \label{thm:b-type-nonempty}
  Interpretations of B types are non-empty, \ie
  \[\forall \tau,\, \interpZ{\tau} \neq \emptyset\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By structural induction on \(\tau\).
  The sets \(\Zint\) and \(\Zbool\) are non-empty by construction;
  \(\emptyset \in \powZ(S)\) for any \(S\);
  the Cartesian product of two non-empty sets is non-empty.
\end{proof}

Nonemptiness ensures that open terms can be given canonical default values, and
that the semantic domain is inhabited at every type.

\subsection{Semantic domain}
\label{subsec:b-sem-domain}

We now define the semantic domain of B terms, \ie the type of interpretations of B terms.
As mentioned already, the B mathematical language is rooted in set theory, so the semantic domain
is naturally expressed over set-theoretic constructions.
Our interpretation of B terms slightly diverges from the standard description given in the
B-Book~\cite{abrial1996bbook} in that we constrain the semantic domain to carry intrinsic B types,
as motivated earlier in \Cref{rem:b-constraint-on-V}, to obtain denotations that are only
defined for \emph{well-typed} values with respect to our type system.
The reason for this design choice is to avoid set-theoretic pathologies related to ill-typed terms,
and provide baked-in typing constraints directly in the semantics, thus circumventing some
well-definedness side conditions---actually, all conditions related to type-correctness---in the
denotation clauses.
This yields a \emph{correct-by-construction} design, carrying typing as an invariant of the semantic domain rather than of the syntax---a discipline in the spirit of type- and scope-safe formalizations~\cite{allais2018syntaxes}.

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:b-typecorrectness-only}
  This design choice only removes case analysis on type-correctness from the denotation clauses, but does not eliminate the need for semantic well-definedness side conditions related to partial operations, like in domain-application or cardinality, which are still present in the semantics and are discussed below.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Semantic domain][B.Dom][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.Dom]
  \label{def:b-dom}
      The semantic domain is the following dependent sum type:
  \[
    \regnotation{\BDom} \triangleq
    \sum_{X : \mathsf{ZFSet}}\sum_{\tau : \mathsf{BType}}\,
    X \in \interpZ{\tau}
  \]
\end{definition}

Elements in \(\BDom\) are therefore dependent triples, denoted \(\regnotation{\denval{X}[\tau]}\),
containing a ZFC set \(X\), a B type \(\tau\) and a proof \(\pf{\tau}{X} : X \in \interpZ{\tau}\).

\begin{remark}
  Although \(\BDom\) is written as a sum indexed by \emph{all} of \(\mathsf{ZFSet}\)---a collection that forms a proper class, not a set, in ZFC---this poses no difficulty in Lean's dependent type theory: \(\BDom\) is a well-formed \emph{type} (a \(\Sigma\)-type), living one universe above \(\mathsf{ZFSet}\). Only its components---the set \(X\), the type \(\tau\), and the membership proof---remain at the set level.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Denoting terms versus typing derivations}
\label{subsec:b-denotation-design}

Baking typing into \(\BDom\) (\Cref{def:b-dom}) is a deliberate design decision, and
the discarded alternative deserves to be made explicit, namely what it recurses on: whether
typing is supplied to the denotation as a separate derivation, or carried by the values
it produces. The same discipline governs the target language, so the discussion below
transfers verbatim to the SMT semantic domain (\Cref{subsec:smt-sem-domain}).

\paragraph{Denoting upon typing derivations.}
One option keeps typing and denotation as separate layers: the denotation recurses on a
typing derivation and returns a bare set,
\[
  \interpB{\cdot} : (t : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\ \mathsf{ZFSet})
    \to (h : \batype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}) \rightharpoonup \mathsf{ZFSet},
\]
where the type \(\tau\) is supplied by the derivation \(h\) and then discarded by the
output. The type system and the semantics are thereby fully decoupled, and each clause
is pure set theory that computes no type. This modularity incurs a twofold cost. First,
recursing on a proof-relevant derivation is a mechanization burden: the derivation must be
threaded through every clause, and one must \emph{separately} prove that the resulting
set does not depend on the chosen derivation, lest the meaning of a term be contingent
on how it is typed. Second, the output is a bare set, so the basic guarantee ``a term of
type \(\tau\) has a denotation within \(\interpZ{\tau}\)'' becomes an external lemma that must be
re-established at every use site: lifted operations such as addition, multiplication, and
set membership each require a separate proof that their arguments carry the expected type
and that the result lands in the expected codomain. The clauses then accumulate
type-correctness side conditions that obscure the genuine partiality of the semantics.

\paragraph{Denoting terms into a type-carrying domain.}
The option we retain carries typing in the codomain: the denotation maps a term to a
value of \(\BDom\), \ie\ a triple \(\denval{X}[\tau]\) bundling a set, its type, and a
membership proof \(\pf{\tau}{X}\), and recurses on the term alone. This pays a single,
local price---\emph{redundancy}: the type \(\tau\) is both carried by the value and
recomputed by the recursion, duplicating information the type system already fixes. In
exchange, the denotation recurses structurally on the \emph{term}, with no reference to
derivations; every value is well-typed by construction, with its membership proof
\(\pf{\tau}{X}\) in tow; and all type-correctness side conditions vanish from the
clauses, leaving only the genuine well-definedness conditions of partial operations
(\Cref{rem:b-typecorrectness-only}).

\paragraph{Rationale.}
We retain the type-carrying domain because its redundancy is exactly what the metatheory
turns into a guarantee rather than a liability. Type adequacy
(\Cref{thm:b-denote-type-det}) shows that whenever a term denotes, the type carried by
its denotation coincides with the one assigned by any typing derivation; totality
(\Cref{thm:b-denote-complete}) establishes the converse, that well-typed terms denote at
the expected type. Together they prove that set-level well-formedness and typing
derivations describe the very same terms---the bridge on which the certified encoding of
\Cref{ch:beer} ultimately rests. The carried type is thus never in conflict with the
derivable one, and in return the semantics becomes a structural, side-condition-free
function whose every output is certified well-typed. This realizes, at the level of the
denotation, the \emph{correct-by-construction} discipline announced in
\Cref{subsec:b-sem-domain}: well-typedness is maintained as an invariant of the semantic
values rather than reconstructed after the fact.

\begin{note}
  In the sense of Reynolds~\cite{reynolds2000meaning}, the type interpretation
  \(\interpZ{\cdot}\) is \emph{extrinsic}: each type denotes a subset \(\interpZ{\tau}\)
  of the single universe \(\mathsf{ZFSet}\), and a value's well-typedness is the
  membership assertion \(X \in \interpZ{\tau}\). The type-carrying domain \(\BDom\)
  internalizes this assertion as a constructive proof \(\pf{\tau}{X}\), whereas the
  discarded alternative---denoting typing derivations---is closer to the
  \emph{intrinsic} reading, in which meaning is assigned to derivations. The
  correspondence is by analogy, neither design being a pure instance; adequacy
  (\Cref{thm:b-denote-type-det}) and totality (\Cref{thm:b-denote-complete}) reconcile
  the two.
\end{note}

\subsection{Denotation}
\label{subsec:b-denote}

As motivated in \Cref{subsec:phoas-for-semantics}, we now use PHOAS representation to define the semantics of B.
The denotation function is therefore a partial function---due to B containing partial constructs requiring well-definedness side conditions---over PHOAS terms parameterized by the semantic domain.

\begin{definition}[Denotation][B.denote][B/SemanticsPHOAS.html\#B.denote]
  \label{def:b-denote}
  The denotation function is defined by structural recursion over PHOAS terms as the partial function
  \[
    \begin{array}{rcccc}
      \regnotation{\interpB{\cdot}} & : & \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\BDom & \rightharpoonup & \BDom     \\
                      &   & t                                 & \mapsto         & \denval{X}[\tau]
    \end{array}
  \]
\end{definition}
The equations defining the denotation function are detailed below, and written as \(\interpB{t} \triangleq \denval{X}[\tau]\) for each term constructor.
In each case, the construction of the proof \(\pf{\tau}{X}\) is detailed.

% In the clauses below, \(\throw\) is used when denotation cannot be defined due to a failure of a well-definedness side condition.

\paragraph{Literals and constants.}
The immediate consequence of our choice of representation is that variables denote themselves.
For any variable \(\var{v} : \BDom\),
\[
  \interpB{\var{v}} \triangleq \var{v}
\]
For any integer \(n\) and Boolean \(b\),
\[
  \interpB{n} \triangleq \denval{\zop{n}}[\intB],\quad
  \interpB{b} \triangleq \denval{\zop{b}}[\boolB]
\]
where $\zop{n}$ and $\zop{b}$ are the respective ZFC encodings of integers and Booleans.
\begin{proofconstruct}
  The two proofs \(\pf{\intB}{\zop{n}} : \zop{n} \in \Zint\) and \(\pf{\boolB}{\zop{b}} : \zop{b} \in \Zbool\) are trivially given by theorems {\small\leancodeptr{mem\_ofInt\_Int}{ZFLean/Integers.html\#ZFSet.mem_ofInt_Int}} and {\small\leancodeptr{ZFBool.mem\_ofBool\_𝔹}{ZFLean/Booleans.html\#ZFSet.ZFBool.mem_ofBool_𝔹}} respectively.
\end{proofconstruct}

For built-in sets, the denotation is given by the interpretation of their ZFC counterparts:
\[
  \interpB{\ZintB}          \triangleq \denval{\Zint}[\setB\intB],\quad
  \interpB{\ZboolB}         \triangleq \denval{\Zbool}[\setB\boolB]
\]

\begin{proofconstruct}
  \(\pf{\setB\intB}{\Zint} : \Zint \in \powZ(\Zint)\) and \(\pf{\setB\boolB}{\Zbool} : \Zbool \in \powZ(\Zbool)\) are trivially given by the fact that any set belongs to its own powerset.
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Pairs.}
Given \(\interpB{t} = \denval{X}[\tau]\) and \(\interpB{u} = \denval{Y}[\sigma]\), we define the denotation of B pairs using Kuratowski pairs\footnote{See~\Cref{subsec:zflean-sets}.}:
\[
  \interpB{t \mapletB u} \triangleq \denval{\regnotation{\pairZ{X}{Y}}}[\tau \prodB \sigma]
\]

\begin{proofconstruct}
  Using that \(\interpZ{\tau \prodB \sigma} \triangleq \interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}\),
  \(\pf{\tau \prodB \sigma}{\pairZ{X}{Y}} : \pairZ{X}{Y} \in \interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}\) easily follows from composing the fact that if a pair is in a Cartesian product, then its components are in the respective factors---see~\Cref{lst:pair-proj} or theorem {\small\leancodeptr{ZFSet.pair\_mem\_prod}{Mathlib/SetTheory/ZFC/Basic.html\#ZFSet.pair_mem_prod}}---with the proofs \(\pf{\tau}{X}\) and \(\pf{\sigma}{Y}\).
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Arithmetic.}
Given \(\interpB{t} = \denval{X}[\intB]\) and \(\interpB{u} = \denval{Y}[\intB]\) (when the types do not match \(\intB\), the denotation is undefined), we define the denotation of arithmetic operations by lifting the corresponding ZFC operations:
\[
  \interpB{t \addB u} \triangleq \denval{X \regnotation{\addZ} Y}[\intB],\quad
  \interpB{t \subB u} \triangleq \denval{X \regnotation{\subZ} Y}[\intB],\quad
  \interpB{t \mulB u} \triangleq \denval{X \regnotation{\mulZ} Y}[\intB]
\]

\begin{proofconstruct}
  All three proofs follow from lemma {\small\leancodeptr{overloadBinOp\_mem}{Extra/ZFC_Extra.html\#overloadBinOp_mem}} that provides closure results for overloaded binary operations,
  composed with the proofs \(\pf{\intB}{X}\) and \(\pf{\intB}{Y}\).
  In this case, integers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication, so the resulting denotations are indeed elements of \(\interpZ{\intB} \triangleq \Zint\).
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Boolean operations.}
Given \(\interpB{t} = \denval{X}[\tau]\) and \(\interpB{u} = \denval{Y}[\sigma]\), we define the denotation of Boolean operations by lifting the corresponding ZFC operations, when the types match the expected ones; otherwise, the denotation is undefined.
\begin{align*}
  \intertext{When \(\tau = \intB\) and \(\sigma = \intB\),}
  \interpB{t \leB u}  & \triangleq \denval{X \regnotation{\leqZ} Y}[\boolB]
  \intertext{When \(\tau = \boolB\),}
  \interpB{\notB t}   & \triangleq \denval{\regnotation{\negZ} X}[\boolB]
  \intertext{When \(\tau = \boolB\) and \(\sigma = \boolB\),}
  \interpB{t \andB u} & \triangleq \denval{X \regnotation{\andZ} Y}[\boolB]
  \intertext{When \(\tau = \sigma\),}
  \interpB{t \eqB u}  & \triangleq \denval{X = Y}[\boolB]
  \intertext{When \(\sigma = \setB\tau\),}
  \interpB{t \inB u}  & \triangleq \denval{X \in Y}[\boolB]
\end{align*}

\begin{proofconstruct}
  All proofs follow directly from the corresponding ZFC lemmas about the respective operations being lifted, composed with the proofs \(\pf{\tau}{X}\) and \(\pf{\sigma}{Y}\).
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Set-theoretic constructors.}
Given \(\interpB{S} = \denval{S'}[\setB\tau]\) and \(\interpB{T} = \denval{T'}[\setB\sigma]\), we define the denotation of set-theoretic constructors by lifting the corresponding ZFC operations, when the types match the expected ones; otherwise, the denotation is undefined.
Those equations witness the fact that the B language is rooted in set theory.

\begin{align*}
  \interpB{S \prodB T} & \triangleq \denval{S' \timesZ T'}[\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)] \\
  \interpB{\powB(S)}   & \triangleq \denval{\powZ(S')}[\setB(\setB\tau)]
\end{align*}
\begin{proofconstruct}
  Using that \(\interpZ{\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)} \triangleq \powZ(\interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma})\), we show
  \begin{align*}
    \undernote{S' \timesZ T' \in \interpZ{\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)}}{\pf{\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)}{S' \timesZ T'}} & \iff S' \timesZ T' \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma})                                                          \\[-18pt]
                                                                                                                     & \iff S' \timesZ T' \subseteq \interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}                                                           \\
                                                                                                                     & \iff S' \subseteq \interpZ{\tau} \land T' \subseteq \interpZ{\sigma}                                                           \\
                                                                                                                     & \iff \undernote{S' \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau})}{\pf{\tau}{S'}} \land \undernote{T' \in \powZ(\interpZ{\sigma})}{\pf{\sigma}{T'}}
  \end{align*}
  Likewise, we construct the proof \(\pf{\setB(\setB\tau)}{\powZ(S')}\).
\end{proofconstruct}
When \(\tau = \sigma\),
\begin{align*}
  \interpB{S \cupB T}  & \triangleq \denval{S' \regnotation{\cupZ} T'}[\setB\tau]                   \\
  \interpB{S \capB T}  & \triangleq \denval{S' \regnotation{\capZ} T'}[\setB\tau]
\end{align*}
\begin{proofconstruct}
  \(\pf{\setB\tau}{S' \cupZ T'} : S' \cupZ T' \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau})\) and \(\pf{\setB\tau}{S' \capZ T'} : S' \capZ T' \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau})\) are easily constructed using monotonicity of union and intersection: {\small\leancodeptr{union\_mono}{Extra/ZFC_Extra.html\#union_mono}}, {\small\leancodeptr{inter\_mono}{Extra/ZFC_Extra.html\#inter_mono}}.
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Partial functions.}

Partial function spaces are interpreted as sets of functional relations.
Given \(\interpB{A} = \denval{A'}[\setB\tau]\) and \(\interpB{B} = \denval{B'}[\setB\sigma]\), let
\[
  \mathcal{F} \coloneq \bigl\{ f \in \powZ(A' \timesZ B') \bigm| \leancodeptr{IsPFunc}{ZFLean/Functions.html\#ZFSet.IsPFunc}\ f\ A'\ B'  \bigr\}\]
Then, the denotation is given by
\[
  \interpB{A \pfunB B}
  =
  \denval{\mathcal{F}}[\setB\bigl(\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)\bigr)]
\]
\begin{proofconstruct}
  Using that \emph{partial} functions are covariant in both their domain and codomain---see lemma {\small\leancodeptr{prod\_sep\_is\_pfunc\_mem}{ZFLean/Functions.html\#ZFSet.prod_sep_is_pfunc_mem}}, we show that
  \begin{align*}
    F & \subseteq \bigl\{ f \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma}) \bigm| \leancodeptr{IsPFunc}{ZFLean/Functions.html\#ZFSet.IsPFunc}\ f\ \interpZ{\tau}\ \interpZ{\sigma}  \bigr\} & \text{(using \(\pf{\setB\tau}{A'}\) and \(\pf{\setB\sigma}{B'}\))}                           \\
      & \subseteq \powZ(\interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma})                                                                                                                                & \text{(using \leancodeptr{sep\_subset}{Mathlib/SetTheory/ZFC/Basic.html\#ZFSet.sep_subset})}
  \end{align*}%
  \ie that \(F \in \powZ(\powZ(\interpZ{\tau} \timesZ \interpZ{\sigma})) = \interpZ{\setB(\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma))}\).
\end{proofconstruct}

\paragraph{Partial operations.}
The semantics of partial operations is given by case analysis on the corresponding well-definedness side conditions, and is defined only when these conditions are satisfied.

Function application evaluates by graph lookup, well defined when the function denotes a partial function and the argument is in its domain.
Given \(\interpB{f} = \denval{F}[\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)]\) and \(\interpB{x} = \denval{X}[\tau]\), if \(\mathsf{IsPFunc}\ F\ \interpZ{\tau}\ \interpZ{\sigma}\) and \(X \in \leancodeptr{Dom}{ZFLean/Functions.html\#ZFSet.Dom}(F)\), let \(Y\) be the unique value such that \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F\); the denotation is then given by
\[
  \interpB{f(x)} = \denval{Y}[\sigma]
\]
\begin{proofconstruct}
  Both uniqueness and the fact that \(Y \in \interpZ{\sigma}\) follow from the definition of \(\mathsf{IsPFunc}\).
\end{proofconstruct}

Cardinality is defined under a finiteness condition; it lifts the corresponding ZFC operation.
Given \(\interpB{S} = \denval{S'}[\setB\tau]\) such that \(\leancodeptr{IsFinite}{ZFLean/Functions.html\#ZFSet.IsFinite}\ S'\), we define:
\begin{align*}
  \interpB{\cardB{S}} & \triangleq \denval{\regnotation{\cardZ{S'}}}[\intB]
\end{align*}
\begin{proofconstruct}
  By definition, \(\cardZ{S'}\) is defined, when \(S'\) is finite, as the unique natural number \(n\), lifted as integer, such that there exists a bijection between \(S'\) and the set \(\{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}\); in particular, \(\cardZ{S'} \in \Zint = \interpZ{\intB}\).
\end{proofconstruct}
\paragraph{Binders.}

Binder constructs rely on the PHOAS representation. Set comprehension is easily interpreted as
ZFC separation, lambda abstraction as graph construction---so, an underlying separation---and
universal quantification as bounded infimum---\ie a set-level conjunction folded over the
quantification domain---in the two-element Boolean algebra.

We start with the most straightforward case of set comprehension \(\bcompa{D}{P}\), where \(D\) is a term and \(P\) is a scoped term.
Definedness assumptions are the following:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(\interpB{D} = \denval{\mathcal{D}}[\setB\tau^{\times}]\), where \(\tau^{\times}\) is a product type \(\tau_1 \prodB \cdots \prodB \tau_n\) of the binder's arity;
  \item For any family of variables \(\vec{\var{x}} = (\var{x_1}, \cdots, \var{x_n})\) of the binder's arity such that, for all \(i\), \(\var{x_i}\) carries the B type \(\tau_i\), and such that \(\pairZ{\var{x_1}}{\cdots, \var{x_n}} \in \mathcal{D}\), \(P(\vec{\var{x}})\) has a denotation.
\end{itemize}
We can therefore define the following scoped denotation \(\mathfrak{P} : \mathsf{ZFSet} \to \BDom\) of the body \(P\).
For any set argument \(z\), \(\mathfrak{P}(z)\) is defined by the following case distinction:

\begin{description}
  \item[if \(z \in \mathcal{D}\):] \(z\) can be written as an \(n\)-ary set-level tuple \(\pairZ{z_1}{\cdots, z_n}\).
        It easily follows from \(\pf{\setB\tau^\times}{\mathcal{D}}\) that each \(z_i\) belongs to \(\interpZ{\tau_i}\), thereby constructing \(\pf{\tau_i}{z_i}\).
        By assumption, \(P\) therefore has a denotation for the family of variables \((\denval{z_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\), whose first component is a set \(p_z\), \ie
        \[
          \interpB{P\bigl((\denval{z_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\bigr)} = \denval{p_z}[\tau_{p_z}]
        \]

        In this case, \(\mathfrak{P}(z)\) is simply this denotation of the body:
        \[
          \mathfrak{P}(z) : \BDom \coloneq \denval{p_z}[\tau_{p_z}]
        \]
        \begin{note}
          The type \(\tau_{p_z}\) carried by \(\mathfrak{P}(z)\) plays no role below: the comprehension separates on the \emph{set} component of \(\mathfrak{P}(z)\) alone---its value \(p_z\)---so we need not require \(\tau_{p_z} = \boolB\) here, a fact easily retrieved once typing assumptions are added.
        \end{note}
  \item[otherwise:] we set \(\mathfrak{P}(z) : \BDom \coloneq \denval{\botZ}[\boolB]\) (an arbitrary default; such \(z \notin \mathcal{D}\) are discarded by the separation below).
\end{description}

The denotation of the whole comprehension is then given by separating \(\mathcal{D}\) on those \(z\) whose body denotes \(\topZ\), using ZFC separation (\leancodeptr{.sep}{Mathlib/SetTheory/ZFC/Basic.html\#ZFSet.sep}), or with usual set-builder notation:
\[
  \interpB{\bcompa{D}{P}}
  \;\triangleq\;
  \denval{\{ z \in \mathcal{D} \mid \mathfrak{P}(z) = \topZ \}}[\setB\tau^\times][\pf{\setB\tau^{\times}}{\{ \mathcal{D} \mid \mathfrak{P} = \topZ \}}]
\]

\begin{proofconstruct}
  The proof is straightforward:
  \[
    \{ z \in \mathcal{D} \mid \mathfrak{P}(z) = \topZ \} \subseteq \undernote{\mathcal{D} \subseteq \interpZ{\tau^\times}}{\pf{\setB\tau^\times}{\mathcal{D}}}
    \vspace{-\baselineskip}
  \]
\end{proofconstruct}

For lambda abstraction \(\lambdaB' D \cdot E\), the result is the graph of the function described by
the body. The definition is quite similar to set comprehension, with the technicality that the return type of the body is not known at this point without typing information, so we need to compute it on the fly as we define the denotation of the body. The assumptions are also similar:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(\interpB{D} = \denval{\mathcal{D}}[\setB\tau^{\times}]\) as before;
  \item For any family of variables \(\vec{\var{x}} = (\var{x_1}, \cdots, \var{x_n})\) of the binder's arity such that, for all \(i\), \(\var{x_i}\) carries the B type \(\tau_i\), and such that \(\pairZ{\var{x_1}}{\cdots, \var{x_n}} \in \mathcal{D}\), \(E(\vec{\var{x}})\) has a denotation.
\end{itemize}
We define the following scoped denotation\footnotemark \(\mathcal{E} : \mathsf{ZFSet} \to \mathsf{Prop}\) of the body \(E\), for any set argument \(z\), by distinguishing whether \(\mathcal{D}\) is empty or not:
\footnotetext{
  In contrast to \(\mathfrak{P}\) above, which ranges in \(\BDom\), the scoped denotation \(\mathcal{E}\) ranges in \(\mathsf{Prop}\): it records the \emph{graph} condition ``\(y\) is the image of \(x\)'' rather than a body value, so a proposition is its natural codomain here.
}
\begin{description}
  \item[if \(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\):] in this case, we cannot directly compute the return type of the body, as we have no element in the domain to plug in it; however, we can still define a dummy family of variables from the domain's type, and use it to compute the return type of the body. We have no guarantee that the body has a denotation for this family of variables, and even if it does, the denotation might be ill-typed.
        \begin{note}
          Even though this last remark may seem degenerate or even pathological, it is important to note that it is not. If such an ill-formed term were to occur as sub-calculation in a denotation clause, the denotation of the whole term would simply fail since it would not satisfy well-definedness assumptions, thus remaining equi-consistent with the semantics of B.
        \end{note}
        Such a dummy family can be defined by picking arbitrary elements \(d_i\) in each \(\interpZ{\tau_i}\), which are non-empty by \Cref{thm:b-type-nonempty}, and defining the family as \((\denval{d_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\).
        If \(E\bigl((\denval{d_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\bigr)\) has a denotation whose second component is a B type \(\gamma\), we define the whole denotation of the abstraction as:
        \[
          \interpB{\lambdaB' D \cdot E} \triangleq \denval{\emptyset}[\setB(\setB(\tau^\times \prodB \gamma))]
        \]
        \begin{proofconstruct}
          Trivial (see {\small\leancodeptr{empty\_subset}{Mathlib/SetTheory/ZFC/Basic.html\#ZFSet.empty_subset}}).
        \end{proofconstruct}

  \item[otherwise:] in this case, we pick an arbitrary element \(d \in \mathcal{D}\), which can be written as an \(n\)-ary set-level tuple \(\pairZ{d_1}{\cdots, d_n}\) as before, and we define the family of variables as \((\denval{d_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\).
        By assumption, \(E\) has a denotation for this family of variables, whose second component is a B type \(\gamma\), which is the return type of the body.
        This type \(\gamma\) does not depend on the choice of representative \(d\): every element of \(\mathcal{D}\) induces a family with the \emph{same} intrinsic types \(\vec\tau\), so the body's denotation carries a type fixed by typing rather than by the particular values chosen (a fact recorded globally by type adequacy, \Cref{thm:b-denote-type-det}).

        The scoped denotation \(\mathcal{E}\) of the body is then defined for arbitrary set arguments \(z\) similarly to the set comprehension case.
        The only difference is that \(z\) is expected to be a set-level pair of the form \(z = \pairZ{x}{y}\).
        We therefore, again, distinguish whether \(x\) belongs to \(\mathcal{D}\):
        \begin{description}
          \item[if \(x \in \mathcal{D}\):] we can write \(x\) as an \(n\)-ary set-level tuple \(\pairZ{x_1}{\cdots, x_n}\), where each \(x_i\) belongs to \(\interpZ{\tau_i}\) as before.
                By assumption, \(E\) has a denotation for the family of variables \((\denval{x_i}[\tau_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\), whose first component is a set \(e_x\). We define the denotation of the body at \(z\) as the following equality:
                \[
                  \mathcal{E}(z) : \mathsf{Prop} \coloneq e_x = y
                \]
          \item[otherwise:] we define \(\mathcal{E}(z) : \mathsf{Prop} \coloneq \mathsf{False}\).
        \end{description}
        The denotation of the whole abstraction is then given using \(\mathcal{E}\) by the set-level separation
        \(\Lambda \coloneq \{ z \in \powZ(\interpZ{\tau^\times} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma}) \mid \mathcal{E}(z) \}\):
        \[
          \interpB{\lambdaB' D \cdot E} \triangleq \denval{\Lambda}[\setB(\setB(\tau^\times \prodB \gamma))]
        \]
\end{description}

\begin{proofconstruct}
  Similar to the set comprehension case.
\end{proofconstruct}

Universal quantification is interpreted as the bounded infimum of the
values obtained over the domain, taken in the two-element ZFC Boolean
algebra $\Zbool$.
The assumptions are again similar to the previous cases:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(\interpB{D} = \denval{\mathcal{D}}[\setB\tau^{\times}]\), with \(\tau^\times = \tau_1 \times \cdots \times \tau_n\) as before;
  \item For any family of variables \(\vec{\var{x}} = (\var{x_i})_{1 \leq i \leq n}\) of the binder's arity such that, for all \(i\), \(\var{x_i}\) carries the B type \(\tau_i\), and such that \(\pairZ{\var{x_1}}{\cdots, \var{x_n}} \in \mathcal{D}\), \(P(\vec{\var{x}})\) has a denotation.
\end{itemize}
The scoped denotation \(\mathfrak{P}\) of the body \(P\) is defined exactly as in the set comprehension case.
The intuition is the following: when the domain \(D\) is finite, so is its denotation \(\mathcal{D} = \{e_1, \cdots, e_k\}\). Universal quantification over that domain is then naturally expressed as the following enumerated conjunction:
\[
  \interpB{\forallBa D \cdot\,P} \;=\; \mathfrak{P}(e_1) \andZ \cdots \andZ \mathfrak{P}(e_k) \;=\; \regnotation{\bigandZ}_{i = 1}^k \mathfrak{P}(e_i) \;=\; \bigandZ_{e \in \mathcal{D}} \mathfrak{P}(e)
\]

This enumerated form is only an \emph{intuition} for the finite case, but we genuinely generalize it to arbitrary domains.
The implementation differs from this equation in two negligible technicalities.
When the domain is empty, \ie \(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\), the infimum should be the top element in the algebra, namely \(\topZ\), also expressing vacuous truth; however in Lean, this infimum is defined as quantified intersection, which is defined, in standard set theory, over non-empty families of sets. In our model, quantified intersection over the empty set is defined as the empty set ({\small\leancodeptr{ZFSet.sInter\_empty}{Mathlib/SetTheory/ZFC/Basic.html\#ZFSet.sInter_empty}}).
In order to circumvent this first technicality, we distinguish the empty domain case and return
\(\denval{\topZ}[\boolB][{\small\leancodeptr{zftrue\_mem\_𝔹}{ZFLean/Booleans.html\#ZFSet.ZFBool.zftrue_mem_𝔹}}]\)
directly in that case.
The second technicality is that our model of ZFC does not offer such generalized comprehension, so we write the following quantified intersection over a separation instead:
\[
  \bigandZ_{\mathcal{D}} \mathfrak{P} = \bigcap \{z \in \ZboolB \mid \exists x \in \mathcal{D},\, y = \mathfrak{P}(x) \}
\]
Overall, the denotation of the whole quantification---on non-empty domains---is given by:
\[
  \interpB{\forallBa D \cdot\,P}
  \triangleq
  \denval{\bigcap \{z \in \ZboolB \mid \exists x \in \mathcal{D},\, y = \mathfrak{P}(x) \}}[\boolB][\pf{}{}]
\]

\begin{proofconstruct}
  The proof \(\pf{}{} : \bigcap \{z \in \ZboolB \mid \exists x \in \mathcal{D},\, y = \mathfrak{P}(x) \} \in \interpZ{\boolB}\) naturally follows from the algebra being structurally closed under conjunction.
\end{proofconstruct}

\subsection{Basic properties}
\label{subsec:b-sem-properties}

The denotation enjoys two fundamental properties: \emph{type adequacy} and \emph{totality} for
well-defined terms.
Type adequacy ensures that whenever a term has a denotation, the intrinsic B type carried by its
denotation coincides with the static type assigned by the typing derivation.
Totality then ensures that, under suitable hypotheses on the environment, every well-defined
and well-typed term does indeed denote a value of the expected type.
Overall, these properties resemble the usual soundness and completeness properties of semantics
with respect to a type system.

\begin{remark}
  These properties relate the denotation to our \emph{type system}. A complementary question is its faithfulness to the \emph{official} semantics of the B-Book~\cite{abrial1996bbook}. On the well-typed fragment---the only one that arises in genuine proof obligations, since Atelier~B rejects ill-typed expressions---our clauses follow the B-Book's set-theoretic reading construct by construct, so the two are designed to agree there; any divergence is confined to ill-typed terms, which the type-carrying domain excludes by construction. A formal soundness proof against a mechanised B-Book semantics is left to future work.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Denotation typing-soundness][denote\_welltyped\_eq][B/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#denote_welltyped_eq]
  \label{thm:b-denote-type-det}
  Let \(t : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\BDom\) be a well-typed PHOAS term, and \(\Gamma\) be a
  well-formed typing context. Then, if \(t\) can be assigned a B type in \(\Gamma\), any denotation
  of \(t\) must carry the same type:
  \[
    \batype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau_0} \quad\land\quad
    \interpB{t} = \denval{V}[\tau][\irrelprf] \quad \implies\quad
    \tau = \tau_0
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By induction on \(t\). The key observation is that, B types carried by
  denotations are constructed resursively from subterm denotations by the very same combinator
  that the typing rules use to combine the subterm types.
  The induction therefore reduces to inverting cases one by one, and identifying the type carried by
  denotations with the types assigned by the typing rules.
\end{proof}

In other words, a term cannot denote a value of two distinct types: the static type---provided by
the typing derivation---fully determines the type tag of the denotation whenever the latter exists.
This is particularly relevant in a set-theoretic setting, where the same set can be given multiple
interpretations as a value of different types; for instance, the set
\(\{\emptyset, \{\emptyset\}\}\) is usually identified either with the natural number \(2\), or the
set of Booleans \(\mathbb{B}\), or potentially other sets.

Note that adequacy carries no well-definedness hypothesis: it constrains whatever denotation
happens to exist, whereas well-definedness is what \emph{guarantees} one. This is the content
of completeness, asserting that well-typed, well-defined terms have a denotation, with the
expected type.

\begin{theorem}[Denotation typing-completeness][B.denote\_exists\_of\_typing][B/Reasoning/DenotationTotality.html\#B.denote_exists_of_typing]
  \label{thm:b-denote-complete}
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a well-formed abstract typing context and let
  \(t : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\BDom\) be well-typed, \(\batype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\), and
  well-defined in the sense of \Cref{def:b-welldefined}, \(\WD(t)\).
  Then \(t\) has a denotation carrying type \(\tau\), \ie there exists
  a set \(T \in \interpZ{\tau}\) such that
  \[
    \interpB{t} = \denval{T}[\tau].
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By induction on the typing derivation \(\batype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\).
  We proceed by a case analysis divided into four groups.

  \paragraph{Variables and literals.}
  Trivial, and in each case, the membership proof was constructed in \Cref{subsec:b-denote}.

  \paragraph{Compound non-partial constructors.}
  The induction hypothesis applied to each subterm produces a witness denotation carrying a
  type that matches the typing-rule premise.
  By \Cref{thm:b-denote-type-det}, every case analysis on subterm type tags inside the
  corresponding denotation clause selects the success branch.
  The witness set is then constructed by applying the relevant ZFC closure lemma to the
  subterm witnesses.
  % Cartesian products for maplets, closure of \(\Zint\) and \(\Zbool\) under the
  % arithmetic and Boolean operations for the connectives, monotonicity of union, intersection,
  % Cartesian product, and power set under inclusion for the set constructors, and the closure of
  % partial-function sets under the covariant action of their domain and codomain for partial
  % function spaces.

  \paragraph{Partial operations.}
  For \(\appB' f\,x\), the induction hypothesis gives denotations
  \[
    \interpB{f} = \denval{F}[\setB(\tau \prodB \sigma)]\quad\text{and}\quad
    \interpB{x} = \denval{X}[\tau].
  \]
  The well-definedness assumption supplies a proof that \(F\) is a partial function from
  \(\interpZ{\tau}\) to \(\interpZ{\sigma}\) and a unique witness \(Y\) with \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F\);
  these ensure---by definition of the denotation---that \(\interpB{\appB' f\,x}\) is defined.
  Thus, we take \(Y\) as witness for the denotation of the application, and the proof
  \(\pf{\sigma}{Y} : Y \in \interpZ{\sigma}\) follows directly from
  \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F \subseteq \interpZ{\tau \prodB \sigma}\).

  For \(\cardB{S}\), writing
  \(\interpB{S} = \denval{\mathcal{S}}[\setB\alpha]\), well-definedness supplies the necessary
  finiteness hypothesis that activates the corresponding denotation clause; the witness
  \(\cardZ{\mathcal{S}}\) belongs to \(\Zint\) by its defining properties.

  \paragraph{Binders.}
  We treat the three binder cases uniformly. Inverting the binder typing rule supplies,
  for some arity \(n \geq 1\),
  a family of B types \(\vec\alpha = (\alpha_1,\cdots,\alpha_n)\), a family of B terms
  \(\vec D = (D_1,\cdots,D_n)\), and a body \(\varphi\) (which is either a comprehension predicate,
  a universally quantified body, or an abstraction body, depending on the constructor), with the
  following data, in line with the binder typing rules of \Cref{subsec:b-concrete-typing}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item the bound domain term is the iterated Cartesian product:
          \(D = D^\times \triangleq D_1 \prodB \cdots \prodB D_n\);
    \item each component is well-typed: \(\forall i,\, \batype{\Gamma}{D_i}{\setB\alpha_i}\);
    \item for every family of PHOAS variables
          \(\vec{\var{v}} = (\var{v_1},\cdots,\var{v_n})\) such that each \(\var{v_i}\) carries the B type \(\alpha_i\),
          the body \(\varphi(\vec{\var{v}})\) is well-typed under the extended context \(\Gamma[\vec{\var{v}} : \vec\alpha]\),
          with type \(\boolB\) for set comprehension and universal quantification, and
          some type \(\gamma\) for lambda abstraction.
  \end{itemize}
  Writing \(\alpha^\times \coloneq \alpha_1 \prodB \cdots \prodB \alpha_n\), the typing-rule output
  is \(\setB\alpha^\times\), \(\boolB\), or \(\setB(\alpha^\times \prodB \gamma)\) for set
  comprehension, universal quantification, and lambda abstraction respectively.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 1: domain denotation.}
  Applying the induction hypothesis to each \(D_i\) yields
  \[
    \interpB{D_i} = \denval{\mathcal{D}_i}[\setB\alpha_i][\pf{}{i}] \qquad\text{with }
    \pf{}{i} : \mathcal{D}_i \subseteq \interpZ{\alpha_i}.
  \]
  Writing \(\mathcal{D}^\times \coloneq \mathcal{D}_1 \times \cdots \times \mathcal{D}_n\), we
  obtain a denotation
  \[
    \interpB{D} = \denval{\mathcal{D}^\times}[\setB\alpha^\times],
  \]
  where \(\pf{\setB\alpha^\times}{\mathcal{D}^\times} : \mathcal{D}^\times \subseteq \interpZ{\alpha^\times}\) is obtained from the proofs \((\pf{}{i})_{1\leq i \leq n}\).

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 2: body denotation at typed tuples.}
  By \Cref{thm:b-type-nonempty}, each \(\interpZ{\alpha_i}\) is non-empty and admits a
  canonical default element \(d_i\), which we package into a default
  family \(\vec d = (\denval{d_i}[\alpha_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\) of PHOAS variables whose intrinsic
  types are precisely \(\vec\alpha\).
  More generally, any tuple \(z \in \interpZ{\alpha^\times}\) decomposes as
  \(z = \pairZ{z_1}{\cdots,z_n}\) with each \(z_i \in \interpZ{\alpha_i}\), and induces a typed
  family \(\vec z = (\denval{z_i}[\alpha_i])_{1 \leq i \leq n}\).
  The induction hypothesis applied to \(\varphi\)
  under the extended context \({\Gamma[\vec z : \vec\alpha]}\)---which is well-formed
  because every \(z_i\) carries intrinsic type \(\alpha_i\)---yields:
  \[
    \interpB{\varphi(\vec z)} = \denval{b_z}[\beta_z], \text{ with } b_z =
    \begin{cases}
      \boolB & \text{for comprehension and universal quantification} \\
      \gamma & \text{for lambda abstraction}
    \end{cases}.
  \]
  The same construction applied to \(\vec d\) yields a Boolean (resp.\@ \(\gamma\)-typed) witness
  for the body at the canonical default tuple.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 3: discharging the side conditions of the denotation clause.}
  The denotation function for binders, given in \Cref{subsec:b-denote}, guards its
  output by a side condition (in addition to the existence of \(\mathcal{D}\)), which enforces
  \emph{parametric body denotation}: for every \(z \in \mathcal{D}\), the body
  denotes at the induced family \(\vec z\), and the resulting denotation carries the expected type.
  This condition is satisfied, so the denotation is defined.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Step 4: assembling the witness.}
  Let \(\Phi : \mathsf{ZFSet} \to \mathsf{ZFSet}\) be the scoped denotation of the
  body introduced in \Cref{subsec:b-denote}, defined for \(z \in \mathcal{D}\) by
  \(\Phi(z) \coloneq b_z\) with \(b_z\) given by Step 2 and extended elsewhere by
  default values. The denotation witness is then assembled by ZFC separation as follows.
  \begin{description}
    \item[Set comprehension:] take
          \(T \coloneq \{ z \in \mathcal{D} \mid \Phi(z) = \topZ \}\); since
          \(T \subseteq \mathcal{D} \subseteq \interpZ{\alpha^\times}\), we have
          \(\pf{\setB\alpha^\times}{T} : T \in \powZ(\interpZ{\alpha^\times}) = \interpZ{\setB\alpha^\times}\).
    \item[Universal quantification:] take
          \[
            T \coloneq
            \begin{cases}
              \displaystyle \bigandZ_{z \in \mathcal{D}} \Phi(z) & \text{when } \mathcal{D} \neq \emptyset \\
              \topZ                                              & \text{when } \mathcal{D} = \emptyset
            \end{cases}
          \]
          which lies in \(\Zbool = \interpZ{\boolB}\).
    \item[Lambda abstraction:] to form the codomain type
          \(\setB(\alpha^\times \prodB \gamma)\), the return type \(\gamma\) of the body
          must first be retrieved. When \(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\), this information is
          supplied by Step 2 applied to \(\vec d\), and the denotation is
          \(T \coloneq \emptyset \in \powZ(\interpZ{\alpha^\times \prodB \gamma})\); when
          \(\mathcal{D} \neq \emptyset\), any element \(z \in \mathcal{D}\) provides a
          representative, Step 2 applied to its induced family \(\vec z\) returns
          the type \(\gamma\), and the denotation is the graph
          \[
            T \coloneq \bigl\{\, \pairZ{z}{y} \in \mathcal{D} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma}
            \,\bigm|\, y = \Phi(z) \,\bigr\}.
          \]
          Since \(T \subseteq \mathcal{D} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma}
          \subseteq \interpZ{\alpha^\times} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma}
          = \interpZ{\alpha^\times \prodB \gamma}\), \(T\) lies in
          \(\interpZ{\setB(\alpha^\times \prodB \gamma)}\) by separation.
  \end{description}

  In each case, the constructed \(T\) belongs to \(\interpZ{\tau}\), thereby providing
  \(\pf{\tau}{T}\), which concludes the proof.
\end{proof}

Adequacy and completeness combine into the statement actually consumed downstream: on the
well-typed, well-defined fragment the denotation is a genuine total function into values of
the expected type.

\begin{corollary}[Totality of the denotation][B.denote\_isSome\_of\_typing][B/Reasoning/DenotationTotality.html\#B.denote_isSome_of_typing]
  \label{cor:b-denote-total}
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a well-formed abstract typing context and let \(t : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\BDom\)
  satisfy \(\batype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\) and \(\WD(t)\). Then \(\interpB{t}\) is defined. Consequently,
  the partial map \(\interpB{\cdot}\) restricts to a \emph{total} function on the well-typed,
  well-defined fragment.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The denotation \(\interpB{t} = \denval{T}[\tau]\) supplied by \Cref{thm:b-denote-complete} witnesses
  that \(\interpB{t}\) is defined.
\end{proof}

This total restriction is precisely the regime in which every B proof obligation lives---Atelier~B
rejects ill-typed expressions, and \(\WD\) discharges partiality---and the interface on which the
certified encoding of \Cref{ch:beer} operates.

\subsection{Conclusion}

This chapter has established the formal foundations of the B mathematical language required by the rest of this work.
A dual syntactic representation was introduced: a concrete first-order syntax for parsing proof obligations, and an abstract syntax suited to semantic reasoning without variable-capture issues.
A typing relation forms the static layer; the denotational semantics, built over the ZFLean set-theoretic framework, then rests on a semantic domain with intrinsic B types---a correct-by-construction design that eliminates all type-correctness side conditions from the denotation clauses and confines partiality to genuine semantic conditions, isolated by a well-definedness predicate.
Three properties tie the layers together: typing-soundness (\Cref{thm:b-denote-type-det}) forbids a term from denoting values of two distinct types; typing-completeness (\Cref{thm:b-denote-complete}) makes every well-typed, well-defined term denote a value of its statically assigned type; and totality (\Cref{cor:b-denote-total}) recasts the two as a total denotation function on that fragment.
The syntax, typing, and semantics of this chapter together form the source-language contract against which the certified encoding of \Cref{ch:beer} is verified.
