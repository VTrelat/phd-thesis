\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats-to-validity}

Only cvc5~1.3.4 was evaluated, with model-based quantifier instantiation and a \(5\)\,s internal budget.
Different solver versions, quantifier strategies, or budgets could change both the absolute success rates and the relative ranking of the encodings.

The corpus is large and industrial, but it comes from a single toolchain, its projects contribute unequal numbers of goals, and one component appears in it three times (\Cref{sec:beer-results-alone}); the subject and construct effects may therefore not generalize to other B developments or proof-obligation generators.
Those classes are descriptive in any case: they record what a goal contains, not what makes a solver succeed.

The obligations themselves come without a ground truth.
Earlier releases of this dataset contained obligations a solver could refute; on this one no refutation appeared, which says that neither encoding gave cvc5 one, not that every obligation is provable.

Neither scoring rule is neutral: strict scoring merges encoder failures with solver failures, and verdict-only scoring removes \bmDropped{} goals asymmetrically, so the full-corpus comparison it implies carries a bias whose direction the campaign cannot determine.

Finally, the measured \textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{} binary implements the verified design but extends and optimizes it beyond the fragment covered by the soundness theorem.
The absence of a \smtinl{sat}/\smtinl{unsat} disagreement with ppTrans is reassuring, but it is not a correctness argument: both translators and the goal-alignment machinery used in the campaign are unverified code.
