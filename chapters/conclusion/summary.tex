The B method makes correctness a matter of proof: consistency and refinement are
reduced to proof obligations, and a development is accepted once all of them have
been discharged. Its trustworthiness therefore depends on the trustworthiness of whatever
discharges them.

This thesis follows B proof obligations along two routes and asks, for each, what
a discharged obligation establishes. On the automated route, \beer{} encodes
obligations into SMT-LIB, and the encoding is proved in Lean to preserve meaning,
so that a solver's answer bears on the original obligation. That proof rests on
\zflean{}, which interprets the fragments of B and SMT-LIB formalized here in one
ZFC universe and thereby turns correctness of the encoding into a statement about
preservation of denotations. On the interactive route, \barrel{} presents
obligations as Lean theorems whose proofs are checked by the kernel. It makes
well-definedness of B's partial operators a typing constraint enforced by construction at every
step.
