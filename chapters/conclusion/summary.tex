This thesis examined how B proof obligations can benefit from automated and
interactive theorem proving while preserving a clear and auditable account of
why accepted results can be trusted. It answers this question by separating the
assurance argument into distinct links---the meaning of the source obligation,
its translation to a backend, the reasoning performed there, and the mechanism
that accepts the result---and by stating the guarantees of the automated and
interactive routes independently. The developments formalize and check key
links in this chain, providing a rigorous foundation for combining automation
and interaction in the B method.
