\section{Main results}
\label{sec:main-achievements}

The first result is a common semantic account of a substantial fragment of the B language and a small fragment of the SMT-LIB
language. \zflean{} (\Cref{ch:zflean}) supplies the ZFC universe in which the B and SMT-LIB
formalizations of \Cref{ch:b,ch:smt} are interpreted. Their syntax, typing, and
denotations can therefore be related within one mathematical model. This turns
the correctness of the encoding from an informal comparison of formulas
into a statement about preservation of meaning.

The second result is that this preservation does not require a single fixed
representation for each B value. Type loosening (\Cref{ch:loosening}) organizes compatible target
representations into a decidable order and bridges functional representations and more general relational ones while preserving semantics. On this
basis, the central theorem for \beer{} relates every successful encoding of a
supported, well-typed, and well-defined term to the representation selected by
the encoder, including the declarations and scoping information generated
during translation. For Boolean terms, thus more generally, proof obligations, the result yields preservation of
truth. \textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{} deliberately falls outside this guarantee:
although it relies on the same design as \beer, its broader coverage is experimental and therefore not verified.

The third result concerns the interactive route. \barrel{} represents every partial B operator as a Lean
function whose definedness proof is an explicit argument, so building a such terms requires supplying that evidence first.
Lean enforces this correct-by-construction discipline wherever a term is
elaborated, including inside a proof, so an obligation closed without the
evidence leaves an admission that an axiom check reports. Atelier~B introduces its
well-definedness conditions when it generates the obligations. Any witness
introduced afterwards, during an interactive proof, therefore escapes them;
\Cref{sec:barrel-atelier-comparison} derives falsehood this way. Proof then proceeds as an ordinary Lean development, using B-specific lemmas, and leveraging Mathlib's tactics.

Together, the two routes cover the automatic and the interactive halves of how a B development can be discharged in practice.
