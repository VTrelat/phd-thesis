\section{Perspectives}
\label{sec:future-work}

\subsection{Toward verified proof-obligation generation}

A semantics of B machines and refinement, together with a verified
proof-obligation generator, would connect the term-level models developed in
this thesis to the correctness of complete B developments. Implementing this
generator in Lean would directly benefit \barrel{}: obligations could be produced
over its shallow embedding, and Lean automation could discharge routine
well-definedness conditions as they are generated. Establishing the semantic
correspondence of that embedding would complement the kernel's guarantee about
completed proofs and shrink the trusted base further. The same infrastructure
would serve the automated route, and it would support an embedded B language for
constructing and verifying machines directly in Lean.

\subsection{Strengthening automated proof}

\beer{} emits scripts in the higher-order fragment of
SMT-LIB~\cite{smtlib2.7}: it represents B functions as SMT functions and relies
on \(\lambda\)-terms and partial application rather than on first-order graph
encodings. cvc5 remains the only SMT solver that accepts this input, so the
automated route has a single usable backend. Wider adoption of the higher-order
fragment, and its consolidation in the proposed
SMT-LIB~3.0~\cite{smtlib3proposal}, would open the same encoding to other solvers
and make portfolio use possible. A standardized higher-order core with an
official semantics would also narrow the gap between the fragment formalized in
\Cref{ch:smt} and the standard itself.

SMT-LIB~3.0 goes further and admits sorts indexed by terms. B's own type
discipline has that shape: a B function type is written over two \emph{sets},
whereas its SMT image is a sort over which the corresponding constraints have to
be asserted separately. Indexed sorts would let those constraints move into the
sort, and part of what \Cref{ch:loosening} now reconciles would be settled by
type checking. Whether that is a good trade is open. Expressiveness in the input
language is paid for in the solver, whose effectiveness rests on decidable
fragments and efficient decision procedures.
\Cref{sec:beer-zero-proof} shows the tension from the other side: making the
encoder fast meant removing machinery whose only consumer was the correctness
proof. A direct encoding serves certification; a tuned one serves throughput;
the two need not coincide.

\subsection{Strengthening interactive proof}

\barrel{} places automatic discharge and human guidance in one workspace, acting
on the same goals: B-specific tactics and \leaninl{grind} run first, the
well-definedness theorems they need are shared across an import, timeouts and
recoverable encoder failures stay confined to a single obligation, and the
infoview reports what has been settled while the remainder is still being
processed. The interactive route is therefore more than the fallback of the
automated one; the two alternate inside a single proof. Growing the lemma library
and raising the stack and memory ceilings extend this workspace without changing
its shape, and proof-obligation generation tailored to Lean would extend it
further, by exploiting well-definedness information before the external POG
representation introduces duplication.

The same workspace accommodates AI-assisted reasoning. An assistant may propose
an invariant, a lemma, or a complete script; Lean's kernel decides whether the
proof is accepted, and the definedness discipline of \Cref{sec:barrel-wd}
decides whether the terms it introduces mean anything. Neither decision depends
on the assistant's account of its own reasoning.

These directions all follow from the principle developed throughout this thesis:
automation can be incorporated into a reasoning workflow while the scope and
basis of each accepted result remain explicit.
