\section{Perspectives}
\label{sec:future-work}

\subsection{Toward verified proof-obligation generation}

A semantics of B machines and refinement, together with a verified
proof-obligation generator, would connect the term-level models developed in
this thesis to the correctness of complete B developments. Implementing this
generator in Lean would directly benefit \barrel{}: proof obligations could be
produced over its shallow embedding, while Lean automation could discharge
routine well-definedness conditions as they are generated. Establishing the
semantic correspondence of the shallow embedding would complement the kernel's
guarantee about completed proofs and further reduce the trusted base. The same
infrastructure could serve the automated route and support an embedded B
language for constructing and verifying machines directly in Lean.

\subsection{Broader higher-order SMT support}

\beer{} emits scripts in the higher-order fragment of
SMT-LIB~\cite{smtlib2.7}: it represents B functions as SMT functions and relies
on \(\lambda\)-terms and partial application rather than on first-order graph
encodings. Today cvc5 is the only solver accepting this input, so the automated
route has a single usable backend. Wider adoption of the higher-order fragment,
and its consolidation in the proposed SMT-LIB~3, would open the same encoding to
other solvers and make portfolio use possible. A standardized higher-order core
with an official semantics would also narrow the gap between the fragment
formalized in \Cref{ch:smt} and the standard itself.

\subsection{Strengthening interactive proof}

\barrel{} now shares repeated well-definedness theorems across an import,
combines its B-specific tactics with \leaninl{grind}, confines timeouts and
recoverable encoder failures to one obligation, and reports live progress in
Lean's infoview. Further engineering can broaden the mathematical fragment and
raise the remaining stack and memory ceilings without changing this
architecture. Proof-obligation generation tailored to Lean would go further:
it could exploit well-definedness information before duplication is introduced
by the external POG representation.

The combination of automatic discharge and interactive proof is also a natural
foundation for AI-assisted reasoning: an AI assistant may propose invariants,
lemmas, or proof scripts, while Lean's kernel remains responsible for
acceptance.

These directions all follow from the principle developed throughout this
thesis: automation can be incorporated into a reasoning workflow while the
scope and basis of each accepted result remain explicit.
