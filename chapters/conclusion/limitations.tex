\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The formal B semantics covers the mathematical fragment required by \beer{},
not the complete B method. Machines, substitutions, refinement, and
proof-obligation generation are outside its scope, and the correspondence
between this semantics and the reference account of B has not been mechanized.
The SMT-LIB formalization likewise covers only the fragment required by the
encoder, since formalizing the complete standard would require substantially
more work. Accordingly, the soundness theorem relates the formal source and
target models developed in this manuscript; further links are needed to obtain
an end-to-end result for a B machine processed by Atelier~B.

Coverage also remains limited. The verified \beer{} encoder does not support,
among other constructs, cardinality, minimum, maximum, or substantial parts of
the sequence language. Unsupported inputs are rejected, preserving soundness at
the cost of applicability.
\barrel{} similarly supports a useful but incomplete fragment.
The evaluation in \Cref{ch:evaluation} uses a corpus of industrial proof
obligations, including very large instances, and provides evidence on realistic
and demanding workloads. Its conclusions remain tied to the studied corpus and
refinement example.

The verified \beer{} encoder is only one part of its execution path. In
addition to Lean, the current toolchain trusts the decoder of Atelier~B
proof-obligation files and its elaborations, the printer from abstract
scripts to concrete SMT-LIB, and the solver's unsatisfiability answer.
The composition of the term-level contracts into a complete script theorem is
also a manuscript-level argument rather than a final mechanized theorem.

For \barrel{}, Lean's kernel checks the generated theorem, but Atelier~B's
proof-obligation generation and the faithfulness of the shallow B-to-Lean
embedding remain trusted. Kernel checking can reject an invalid derivation of a
Lean proposition; it cannot establish that this proposition is the correct
translation of the original B obligation. These boundaries limit the present
claims, but also identify precisely which links remain to be verified.
