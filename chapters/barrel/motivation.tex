\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:barrel-introduction}

\Cref{ch:beer} developed the automated route of \Cref{fig:intro-reasoning-routes}: a
verified encoder that turns a B proof obligation into an SMT-LIB script and hands it to a
solver.
That route is one-shot: either the solver answers \texttt{unsat}, or it does not, and the
failure says nothing about the cause.
The obligations it leaves behind are typically the ones carrying the mathematical content
of a refinement, and they have to be taken up by hand.
Atelier~B answers that need with its own interactive prover.
This chapter follows the complementary route and asks what is gained by replacing that
stage---and only that stage---with a general-purpose proof assistant.

\barrel{} is a Lean~4 library, developed jointly with Ghilain Bergeron, that consumes the
artifacts Atelier~B already produces~\cite{bergeron2026barrel,bergeron2026barrelAFADL}.
It imports a machine, refinement, implementation, or system---or a previously generated
proof obligation file---translates every supported obligation into a Lean proposition,
attempts each one with proof-producing automation, and leaves the propositions it cannot
close as ordinary Lean goals.
The user still writes and type-checks the B artifact with Atelier~B, and Atelier~B still
generates the obligations.
A discharged obligation now becomes a named theorem whose proof term the kernel has checked
and whose axiom dependencies can be listed.
Two design decisions shape the rest of the chapter.

\paragraph*{Partiality is made structural.}
Many B operators are partial: \(\minB(S)\) denotes a least element only if one exists, and
\(\appB f\,x\) is meaningful only when \(f\) is functional and \(x\) lies in its domain.
As recalled in \Cref{sec:b-wd}, the B method reads such an operator as a bounded Hilbert
choice and keeps the goal logic total, emitting a separate well-definedness (WD) obligation
for each occurrence of a partial operator; these obligations are generated globally, so the correspondence
between an occurrence and the side condition meant to justify it is left implicit.
\barrel{} takes the opposite stance.
Every partial operator is a Lean function that takes a proof of its definedness as an
explicit argument, so the translated term cannot be built without that evidence---or, when
the evidence does not exist, cannot be built without an admission that the tooling then
reports.
Well-definedness stops being a side obligation and becomes a typing constraint;
\Cref{sec:barrel-wd} develops this and \Cref{sec:barrel-atelier-comparison} shows what it
rules out.

\paragraph*{The embedding is typed and relational.}
\Cref{ch:b,ch:zflean} interpret B in an untyped universe of ZFC sets, which is the right
setting for a soundness proof relating two object languages.
\barrel{} has no such proof to state, and can therefore exploit a resource that
Atelier~B provides for free: the \emph{parent sets} its static well-formedness discipline
already infers for every expression (\Cref{sec:b-typing}).
These are exactly the type annotations needed to land in Mathlib's typed set theory, where
the existing body of results about sets, relations, orders, finiteness, and arithmetic
applies directly.
The two routes therefore diverge immediately below the proof obligation, and they stay
apart: nothing in the interactive route obliges a B function to be anything other than what
it is set-theoretically, a functional relation.

The B method, its refinement discipline, and its mathematical language are covered in
\Cref{sec:b-method} and formalized in \Cref{ch:b}; the Lean features used below are those
of \Cref{sec:lean}.
\Cref{sec:barrel-architecture} places \barrel{} in the Atelier~B workflow;
\Cref{sec:barrel-embedding,sec:barrel-wd} present the typed model of B and the treatment of
partial operators; \Cref{sec:barrel-lean-metaprogramming} explains how obligations become
theorems and delimits the trust boundary;
\Cref{sec:barrel-example,sec:barrel-interface} work through a refinement chain, an
industrial-scale import, and the proof interface.
\Cref{sec:barrel-atelier-comparison} states the guarantee the encoding provides,
\Cref{sec:barrel-related-work} positions the tool, and \Cref{sec:barrel-discussion}
concludes.
The division of work between the two of us follows these layers: the metaprogramming of
\Cref{sec:barrel-lean-metaprogramming}, which turns an obligation into a Lean declaration,
is largely Ghilain Bergeron's, while the typed model of B and the library of facts it rests
on, the automation, and the proof interface are my own.
