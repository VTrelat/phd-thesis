\section{Semantics}
\label{sec:smt-semantics}

As for the source language, the semantics of SMT developed in this work is
\emph{denotational}: each term is assigned a value in the set-theoretic domain of
the ZFLean framework of \Cref{ch:zflean}.
Concrete terms are interpreted after abstraction to PHOAS, as described in
\Cref{subsec:smt-syntax-change-repr}.

\subsection{Interpretation of SMT types}
\label{subsec:smt-type-interp}

We first interpret SMT types as ZFC sets, denoted \(\interpZ{\cdot}\).

\begin{definition}[Type interpretation][SMT.SMTType.toZFSet][SMT/Semantics.html\#SMT.SMTType.toZFSet]
  \label{def:smt-type-interp}
  The function \(\interpZ{\cdot} : \mathsf{SMTType} \to \mathsf{ZFSet}\) is
  defined by structural recursion:
  \[
    \begin{array}{rclcrcl}
      \interpZ{\boolS}              & = & \Zbool, & \qquad &
      \interpZ{\intS}               & = & \Zint,                                  \\[2pt]
      \interpZ{\unitS}              & = & \{\emptyset\}, & \qquad &
      \interpZ{\alpha \timesS \beta}& = & \interpZ{\alpha} \timesZ \interpZ{\beta}, \\[2pt]
      \interpZ{\alpha \toS \beta}   & = & \interpZ{\alpha} \regnotation{\funsZ} \interpZ{\beta}, & \qquad &
      \interpZ{\optS\tau}           & = & \regnotation{\optZ}(\interpZ{\tau}),
    \end{array}
  \]
  where \(\funsZ\) denotes the set of total functions (functional graphs) between
  two sets, and \(\optZ\) the option encoding.
\end{definition}

The option encoding tags its contents with a Boolean discriminant:
\[
  \optZ(S) \;=\; \bigl(\{\regnotation{\botZ}\} \timesZ \{\emptyset\}\bigr)
                 \;\cupZ\; \bigl(\{\topZ\} \timesZ S\bigr),
\]
so that the absent value is \(\regnotation{\noneZ} \triangleq \pairZ{\botZ}{\emptyset}\) and a
present value \(x \in S\) is \(\regnotation{\someZ}\,x \triangleq \pairZ{\topZ}{x}\); the
extractor \(\regnotation{\theZ}\) returns the second component of a present value and a default
otherwise.

As on the source side, the set-theoretic interpretation is appropriate only if
SMT types are inhabited.

\begin{lemma}[Nonemptiness][SMT.SMTType.toZFSet\_nonempty][SMT/Semantics.html\#SMT.SMTType.toZFSet_nonempty]
  \label{thm:smt-type-nonempty}
  Interpretations of SMT types are non-empty: \(\forall \tau,\, \interpZ{\tau} \neq \emptyset\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By structural induction on \(\tau\). The sets \(\Zbool\), \(\Zint\), and
  \(\{\emptyset\}\) are non-empty by construction; the function space is
  non-empty because the constant graph at any codomain element is total; the
  option set contains \(\noneZ\); and the Cartesian product of two non-empty sets
  is non-empty.
\end{proof}

The proof is constructive: it exhibits a canonical inhabitant
\(d_{\tau} \in \interpZ{\tau}\) at each type---the set-theoretic image of the
default term \(\spec{\tau}\) of \Cref{def:smt-default}. These canonical
inhabitants are available as
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.SMTType.defaultZFSet}{SMT/Semantics.html\#SMT.SMTType.defaultZFSet}}
and supply default values for empty binder scopes in the denotation below.

\subsection{Semantic domain}
\label{subsec:smt-sem-domain}

We now define the semantic domain of SMT terms. As for the source language
(\Cref{subsec:b-sem-domain}), we constrain the domain to carry intrinsic sorts,
so that denotations are defined only for \emph{well-sorted} values. This
correct-by-construction design removes all type-correctness side conditions from
the denotation clauses, leaving only the genuine partiality of partial
operations.

\begin{definition}[Semantic domain][SMT.Dom][SMT/Semantics.html\#SMT.Dom]
  \label{def:smt-dom}
      The semantic domain is the dependent sum
  \[
    \mathsf{Dom} \triangleq
    \sum_{X : \mathsf{ZFSet}}\sum_{\tau : \mathsf{SMTType}}\, X \in \interpZ{\tau}.
  \]
\end{definition}

Elements of \(\mathsf{Dom}\) are dependent triples, denoted \(\denval{X}[\tau]\),
consisting of a ZFC set \(X\), an SMT type \(\tau\), and a proof
\(\pf{\tau}{X} : X \in \interpZ{\tau}\). As before, we write ``\(\irrelprf\)'' for
the proof component when it is irrelevant. The intrinsic sort of an element is its
second component; the canonical inhabitant of \Cref{thm:smt-type-nonempty}
equips \(\mathsf{Dom}\) with a representative \(\denval{d_\tau}[\tau]\) of every
sort, which is precisely the \emph{richness} required by the determinism result
of \Cref{thm:smt-typing-det}.

\subsection{Denotation}
\label{subsec:smt-denote}

The denotation is a partial function over PHOAS terms parameterized by the
semantic domain, following the trick of \Cref{subsec:phoas-for-semantics}.

\begin{definition}[Denotation][SMT.denote][SMT/Semantics.html\#SMT.denote]
  \label{def:smt-denote}
    The denotation function is defined by structural recursion over PHOAS terms as
  the partial function
  \[
    \regnotation{\interpS{\cdot}} : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\mathsf{Dom} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{Dom}.
  \]
\end{definition}

The defining equations are detailed below, written \(\interpS{t} \triangleq
\denval{X}[\tau]\); in each case the membership proof \(\pf{\tau}{X}\) is
constructed alongside the value.

\paragraph{Variables and literals.}
The PHOAS trick makes variables denote themselves: for \(v : \mathsf{Dom}\),
\(\interpS{v} \triangleq v\). For an integer \(n\), a Boolean \(b\), and the unit
value,
\[
  \interpS{n} \triangleq \denval{\zop{n}}[\intS],\quad
  \interpS{b} \triangleq \denval{\zop{b}}[\boolS],\quad
  \interpS{\unitvalS} \triangleq \denval{\emptyset}[\unitS],
\]
where \(\zop{n}\) and \(\zop{b}\) are the ZFC encodings of integers and Booleans.

\paragraph{Pairs and projections.}
Given \(\interpS{t} = \denval{X}[\alpha]\) and \(\interpS{s} = \denval{Y}[\beta]\),
\[
  \interpS{{\pairS{t}{s}}} \triangleq \denval{\pairZ{X}{Y}}[\alpha \timesS \beta],
\]
using Kuratowski pairs. Projections are defined when the argument has a pair
sort: given \(\interpS{t} = \denval{T}[\alpha \timesS \beta]\),
\[
  \interpS{\fstS t} \triangleq \denval{\fstZ(T)}[\alpha], \qquad
  \interpS{\sndS t} \triangleq \denval{\sndZ(T)}[\beta],
\]
where \(\fstZ, \sndZ\) are the set-theoretic projections; the membership proofs
follow from \(T \in \interpZ{\alpha} \timesZ \interpZ{\beta}\).

\paragraph{Arithmetic.}
Given \(\interpS{t} = \denval{X}[\intS]\) and \(\interpS{s} = \denval{Y}[\intS]\)
(otherwise the denotation is undefined), arithmetic lifts the corresponding ZFC
operations:
\[
  \interpS{t \addS s} \triangleq \denval{X \addZ Y}[\intS],\quad
  \interpS{t \subS s} \triangleq \denval{X \subZ Y}[\intS],\quad
  \interpS{t \mulS s} \triangleq \denval{X \mulZ Y}[\intS],
\]
and comparison yields a Boolean: \(\interpS{t \leS s} \triangleq \denval{X \leqZ Y}[\boolS]\).

\paragraph{Boolean connectives.}
Given denotations of the expected sorts, the primitive connectives lift their ZFC
counterparts in the two-element Boolean algebra \(\Zbool\):
\begin{align*}
  \interpS{t \eqS s}  & \triangleq \denval{X \eqZ Y}[\boolS] && \text{when the two sorts agree,} \\
  \interpS{t \andS s} & \triangleq \denval{X \andZ Y}[\boolS] && \text{when both sorts are } \boolS, \\
  \interpS{\notS t}   & \triangleq \denval{\negZ X}[\boolS] && \text{when the sort is } \boolS.
\end{align*}
Disjunction, implication, and the existential quantifier are interpreted through
their definitions as derived forms (\Cref{def:smt-derived}).

\paragraph{Options and unit.}
Given \(\interpS{t} = \denval{T}[\tau]\), the option constructors are interpreted
by the tagged encoding of \Cref{def:smt-type-interp}:
\[
  \interpS{\someS t} \triangleq \denval{\someZ\,T}[\optS\tau],\qquad
  \interpS{\noneS_{\tau}} \triangleq \denval{\noneZ}[\optS\tau].
\]
The extractor is defined when its argument has an option sort: given
\(\interpS{t} = \denval{T}[\optS\tau]\),
\[
  \interpS{\theS t} \triangleq \denval{\theZ\,T}[\tau],
\]
where \(\theZ\) returns the wrapped value of a present option and the canonical
default \(d_{\tau}\) otherwise; the latter case is harmless, since the adequacy
results of \Cref{subsec:smt-sem-properties} guarantee a present value whenever
the source obligation is well-defined.

\paragraph{Distinctness.}
Distinctness of a family is the iterated, pairwise conjunction asserting that no
element equals any later one, folded in \(\Zbool\):
\begin{equation*}
  \mathsf{distinct}(\,) \triangleq \topZ, \qquad
  \mathsf{distinct}(x, \vec x) \triangleq (x \notin \vec x) \andZ \mathsf{distinct}(\vec x),
\end{equation*}
and \(\interpS{\distinctS \vec t} \triangleq \denval{\mathsf{distinct}(\interpS{t_1}, \dots, \interpS{t_k})}[\boolS]\)
when every \(t_i\) denotes.

\paragraph{Application.}
Application is the only genuinely partial operation, and is interpreted by graph
lookup. Given \(\interpS{f} = \denval{F}[\sigma \toS \tau]\) and
\(\interpS{x} = \denval{X}[\sigma']\), the denotation is defined when the sorts
match (\(\sigma = \sigma'\)), \(F\) is a functional graph from
\(\interpZ{\sigma}\) to \(\interpZ{\tau}\), and \(X\) lies in its domain; letting
\(Y\) be the unique value with \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F\),
\[
  \interpS{\appSa{f}{x}} \triangleq \denval{Y}[\tau].
\]
Both the uniqueness of \(Y\) and the membership \(Y \in \interpZ{\tau}\) follow
from \(F\) being a functional graph.

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:smt-wd}
  This is the only well-definedness side condition in the SMT semantics. The
  source language additionally requires finiteness for cardinality, minimum, and
  maximum (\Cref{sec:b-wd}); SMT-LIB has no such operators in the fragment
  considered, and its function spaces are total, so the denotation is partial
  only at application---and even there, the adequacy results below show that the
  side condition is automatically satisfied for well-typed terms whose function
  argument is supplied within its domain.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Binders.}
Binder constructs rely on the PHOAS representation, exactly as in
\Cref{subsec:b-denote}. For a binder of arity \(n\) with type vector
\(\vec\tau\), write \(\mathcal{D} \coloneq \interpZ{\vec\tau^{\timesS}}\) for the
carrier of the argument tuple. Each \(z \in \mathcal{D}\) decomposes into
components \(z_i \in \interpZ{\tau_i}\) and induces a family of domain values
\(\vec z = (\denval{z_i}[\tau_i])_i\); the scoped denotation of the body is the
function on \(\mathcal{D}\) obtained by interpreting the body at \(\vec z\).

\emph{Lambda abstraction} denotes as the graph of the scoped body. Writing
\(\Phi(z)\) for the (set component of the) body denotation at the tuple \(z\), and
\(\gamma\) for its sort,
\[
  \interpS{\lamS \vec\tau \cdot e}
  \triangleq
  \denval{\{\, \pairZ{z}{y} \in \mathcal{D} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma} \mid y = \Phi(z) \,\}}[\vec\tau^{\timesS} \toS \gamma].
\]
The return sort \(\gamma\) is computed on the fly from the body at the canonical
default tuple \((\denval{d_{\tau_i}}[\tau_i])_i\), which exists by
\Cref{thm:smt-type-nonempty}; this also covers the case
\(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\), where no genuine argument is available.

\emph{Universal quantification} denotes as the bounded infimum of the body values
over the domain, taken in the two-element Boolean algebra:
\[
  \interpS{\forallS \vec\tau \cdot P}
  \triangleq
  \denval{\bigcap \{\, y \in \Zbool \mid \exists z \in \mathcal{D},\ y = \Phi(z) \,\}}[\boolS].
\]
When \(\mathcal{D}\) is finite, this is the finite conjunction
\(\bigandZ_{z \in \mathcal{D}} \Phi(z)\); the intersection form extends it to
arbitrary domains, and the empty-domain case yields \(\topZ\), expressing vacuous
truth.

\emph{Existential quantification} is the derived form
\(\existsS \vec\tau \cdot P \triangleq \notS(\forallS \vec\tau \cdot \notS P)\),
and denotes accordingly.

\subsection{Basic properties}
\label{subsec:smt-sem-properties}

The denotation enjoys the same two fundamental properties as on the source side:
\emph{type adequacy} and \emph{totality} for well-typed terms, together
resembling the soundness and completeness of the semantics with respect to the
type system.

\paragraph{Type adequacy.}
Adequacy states that whenever a term denotes, the intrinsic sort carried by its
denotation coincides with the sort assigned by any typing derivation. It rests on
the determinism of the type system (\Cref{thm:smt-typing-det}).

\begin{theorem}[Denotation typing-soundness][SMT.PHOAS.denote\_welltyped\_eq][SMT/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#SMT.PHOAS.denote_welltyped_eq]
  \label{thm:smt-denote-type-det}
  Let \(t : \mathsf{PHOAS.Term}\,\mathsf{Dom}\) be a term admitting a typing
  \(\satype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau_0}\) under a well-formed context \(\Gamma\). If
  \(\interpS{t} = \denval{V}[\tau][\irrelprf]\), then \(\tau = \tau_0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on \(t\). The key observation is that the sort carried by a
  denotation is constructed from the subterm denotations by the very same
  combinator that the typing rules use to combine subterm sorts. The induction
  therefore reduces to inverting each constructor and identifying the carried
  sort with the assigned one: literals and the unit value carry fixed sorts;
  variables read their sort from the well-formed context; compound non-binders
  combine the sorts of their subterms, which match by the induction hypotheses.
  Universal quantification and the Boolean connectives always carry \(\boolS\).
  For lambda abstraction, the codomain sort is the body sort computed by the
  denotation, which the induction hypothesis identifies with the sort \(\gamma\)
  of the typing premise; the argument sort is the same tuple sort in both, so the
  two function sorts agree.
\end{proof}

In other words, a term cannot denote a value of two distinct sorts: the static
sort fully determines the sort tag of the denotation whenever the latter exists.
This matters in a set-theoretic setting, where the same set may inhabit several
sorts---for instance the encoding of an integer may coincide with that of an
option or a pair.

\paragraph{Totality.}
Totality is the converse existence statement: well-typed terms have a denotation,
of the expected sort. As on the source side, the abstraction of a concrete term
denotes provided the renaming is sort-compatible with the typing context and
covers the term's free variables. We record the two conditions.

\begin{definition}[Covering and sort-compatible renaming][SMT.RenamingContext.CoversFV, .RespectsTypeContext][
    SMT/Reasoning/Defs.html\#SMT.RenamingContext.CoversFV,
    SMT/Reasoning/Defs.html\#SMT.RenamingContext.RespectsTypeContext]
  \label{def:smt-renaming-conditions}
  A renaming \(\Delta : \mathcal{V} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{Dom}\) \emph{covers}
  a term \(t\) if it is defined on every free variable of \(t\), and is
  \emph{sort-compatible} with a context \(\Gamma\) if, whenever
  \(\Gamma(v) = \tau\), the value \(\Delta(v)\) is defined and carries intrinsic
  sort \(\tau\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Denotation typing-completeness][SMT.RenamingContext.denote\_exists\_of\_typing][SMT/Reasoning/Basic/DenotationTotality.html\#SMT.RenamingContext.denote_exists_of_typing]
  \label{thm:smt-denote-complete}
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a concrete typing context, \(t\) a concrete SMT term with
  \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\), and \(\Delta\) a renaming that covers \(t\) and is
  sort-compatible with \(\Gamma\). Then \(\aterm{t}{\Delta}\) has a denotation
  carrying sort \(\tau\): there is a set \(T \in \interpZ{\tau}\) with
  \[
    \interpS{\aterm{t}{\Delta}} = \denval{T}[\tau].
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the typing derivation \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\). The argument
  splits into four groups of cases.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Variables and literals.}
  For a variable, sort-compatibility supplies a domain value \(\Delta(v)\) of
  sort \(\tau\); abstraction maps \(\mathsf{var}\ v\) to it, and a variable
  denotes itself. Literals and the unit value are immediate, with the membership
  proofs constructed in \Cref{subsec:smt-denote}.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Compound non-partial constructors.}
  The induction hypothesis applied to each subterm yields a witness denotation
  whose sort matches the corresponding typing premise. By type adequacy
  (\Cref{thm:smt-denote-type-det}), each sort case analysis inside the denotation
  clause selects the success branch. The witness set is then assembled by the
  relevant ZFC closure lemma: Cartesian products and projections for pairs,
  closure of \(\Zint\) and \(\Zbool\) under the arithmetic and Boolean
  operations, the tagged encoding for the option constructors, and the folded
  conjunction for distinctness. The derived connectives \(\orS\), \(\impS\), and
  \(\existsS\) reduce to their definitions and are covered by the corresponding
  primitive cases.

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Application.}
  The induction hypothesis gives
  \(\interpS{f} = \denval{F}[\sigma \toS \tau]\) and
  \(\interpS{x} = \denval{X}[\sigma]\). Since \(F \in \interpZ{\sigma \toS \tau} =
  \interpZ{\sigma} \funsZ \interpZ{\tau}\), it is a functional graph with domain
  \(\interpZ{\sigma}\), which contains \(X\); the well-definedness side condition
  of the application clause is therefore satisfied, and the unique \(Y\) with
  \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F\) provides the witness, with
  \(\pf{\tau}{Y} : Y \in \interpZ{\tau}\) following from
  \(\pairZ{X}{Y} \in F \subseteq \interpZ{\sigma} \timesZ \interpZ{\tau}\).

  \smallskip\noindent\emph{Binders.}
  We treat the three binder cases uniformly. Inversion of the binder typing rule
  supplies, for some arity \(n \geq 1\), a type vector \(\vec\tau\) and a body
  \(\varphi\) (a comprehension predicate, a quantified body, or an abstraction
  body) such that the body is well-typed under \(\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]\) for
  every family of variables \(\vec v\) carrying the sorts \(\vec\tau\), with sort
  \(\boolS\) for universal quantification and some \(\gamma\) for lambda
  abstraction. The argument proceeds in four steps.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), nosep]
    \item \emph{Domain.} The carrier \(\mathcal{D} = \interpZ{\vec\tau^{\timesS}}\)
          is the product of the component carriers; each tuple
          \(z \in \mathcal{D}\) decomposes into components
          \(z_i \in \interpZ{\tau_i}\).
    \item \emph{Body at typed tuples.} Each \(z\) induces a family
          \(\vec z = (\denval{z_i}[\tau_i])_i\) of the prescribed sorts; the
          induction hypothesis, applied to the body under
          \(\Gamma[\vec z:\vec\tau]\)---well-formed because each \(z_i\) carries
          sort \(\tau_i\)---yields a body denotation of sort \(\boolS\)
          (respectively \(\gamma\)). The canonical default tuple of
          \Cref{thm:smt-type-nonempty} provides the same data when
          \(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\).
    \item \emph{Side conditions.} The denotation clause for binders is guarded by
          parametric body denotation (the body denotes, with the expected sort,
          at every typed tuple); Steps (1)--(2) and adequacy discharge this guard,
          so the denotation is defined.
    \item \emph{Witness.} Writing \(\Phi\) for the scoped body denotation, the
          witness is assembled by separation: the graph
          \(\{\, \pairZ{z}{y} \in \mathcal{D} \timesZ \interpZ{\gamma} \mid y =
          \Phi(z) \,\}\) for lambda, which lies in
          \(\interpZ{\vec\tau^{\timesS} \toS \gamma}\); the bounded infimum
          \(\bigcap\{\, y \in \Zbool \mid \exists z \in \mathcal{D},\ y = \Phi(z)
          \,\}\) for universal quantification (and \(\topZ\) when
          \(\mathcal{D} = \emptyset\)), which lies in \(\Zbool\); and the negation
          of the corresponding universal denotation for the existential.
  \end{enumerate}
  In each case the constructed witness belongs to \(\interpZ{\tau}\), providing
  \(\pf{\tau}{T}\) and concluding the proof.
\end{proof}

Two corollaries record the immediate consequences used downstream: a well-typed
term with a sort-compatible, covering renaming always denotes
({\small\leancodeptr{SMT.RenamingContext.denote\_isSome\_of\_typing}{SMT/Reasoning/Basic/DenotationTotality.html\#SMT.RenamingContext.denote_isSome_of_typing}}),
and the sort of its denotation is the statically assigned one
({\small\leancodeptr{SMT.RenamingContext.denote\_type\_of\_typing}{SMT/Reasoning/Basic/DenotationTotality.html\#SMT.RenamingContext.denote_type_of_typing}}).

The syntax, typing, and semantics of this chapter form the target-language
contract against which the encoder of \Cref{ch:beer} is proved correct: every
B proof obligation is mapped to an SMT term whose denotation, in the shared ZFC
universe, equals that of its source.
