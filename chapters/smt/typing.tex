\section{Type System}
\label{sec:smt-typing}

We introduce a type system for SMT terms, both in the concrete and abstract syntax.
This section describes the typing rules for concrete and abstract SMT syntax,
over the type system of \Cref{def:smt-types}.
Then, we prove basic structural properties of both type systems and connect them
via a context abstraction that lifts concrete typing derivations to the PHOAS setting.

Unlike the B mathematical language, SMT-LIB is intrinsically many-sorted: its
type system is part of the language definition rather than an auxiliary device.
It nonetheless serves the same two purposes as on the source side, as detailed in \Cref{sec:b-typing}.


\subsection{Concrete typing rules}
\label{subsec:smt-concrete-typing}

Concrete typing is defined over the first-order syntax of
\Cref{def:smt-basic-syntax}. The rules are syntax-directed and form judgments
\(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\), where \(\Gamma : \mathcal{V} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{SMTType}\) is a
typing context, \ie a map from concrete variables to SMT types \leaninl{SMTType},
\(t\) is a concrete SMT term, and \(\tau\) is an SMT type.
The binder rules share a side condition, analogous to \Cref{eq:def-bind} but expressed with sort
annotations rather than a domain set.
For a body \(t\), bound variables \(\vec v\), and SMT types \(\vec\tau\), we write
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathsf{Bind}(\Gamma,\vec v,\vec\tau,t) \coloneq
      &\underbrace{(\forall v \in \vec v,\; v \notin \Gamma)}_{\text{bound variables are fresh for }\Gamma}
      \;\land\; \underbrace{(\forall v \in \vec v,\; v \notin \mathsf{bv}(t))}_{\text{not already bound in the body}} \\[1ex]
      \land{} &\underbrace{0 < |\vec v|}_{\text{non-empty}}
      \;\land\; \underbrace{|\vec v| = |\vec\tau|}_{\text{equal length}}
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{Bind\textsuperscript{S} def.}
  \label{eq:def-bind-smt}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathsf{bv}(t)\) denotes the bound variables of \(t\). We further keep the notation
\(\tau^{\times} \coloneq \tau_1 \timesS \cdots \timesS \tau_n\) for the
right-nested pair SMT type associated with \(\vec\tau\).

\begin{definition}[Concrete typing rules][SMT.Typing][SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing]
  \label{def:smt-concrete-typing}
    The inductive typing predicate \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\) is defined
  structurally on \(t\) by the following rules.
  \paragraph{Atoms and application.}
  \begin{mathpar}
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{var}
      {\Gamma(v)=\tau}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\mathsf{var}\ v}{\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{int}
      { }
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\mathsf{int}\ n}{\intS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{bool}
      { }
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\mathsf{bool}\ b}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{app}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{f}{\tau \toS \sigma} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{x}{\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\appS f\ x}{\sigma}}
  \end{mathpar}
  \paragraph{Binders.}
  \begin{mathpar}
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{lambda}
      {\mathsf{Bind}(\Gamma,\vec v,\vec\tau,t) \quad \stype{\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]}{t}{\gamma}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\lamS \vec v : \vec\tau \cdot t}{\tau^{\times} \toS \gamma}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{forall}
      {\mathsf{Bind}(\Gamma,\vec v,\vec\tau,P) \quad \stype{\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]}{P}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\forallS \vec v : \vec\tau \cdot P}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{exists}
      {\mathsf{Bind}(\Gamma,\vec v,\vec\tau,P) \quad \stype{\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]}{P}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\existsS \vec v : \vec\tau \cdot P}{\boolS}}
  \end{mathpar}
  where \(\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]\) denotes the context extension of \(\Gamma\) with the bindings
  \(v_i : \tau_i\) for each \(v_i\) in \(\vec v\) and corresponding \(\tau_i\) in \(\vec\tau\), and
  \(\tau^{\times}\) is the right-nested pair SMT type associated with \(\vec\tau\).
  \paragraph{Logic.}
  \begin{mathpar}
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{eq}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\tau} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \eqS y}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{and}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\boolS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \andS y}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{or}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\boolS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \orS y}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{not}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\notS x}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{imp}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\boolS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\boolS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \impS y}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{ite}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{c}{\boolS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{e}{\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\iteS\ c\ t\ e}{\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{distinct}
      {\forall t \in \vec t,\; \stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\distinctS \vec t}{\boolS}}
  \end{mathpar}
  \paragraph{Options and pairs.}
  \begin{mathpar}
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{some}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\someS t}{\optS\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{none}
      { }
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\asS\ \noneS\ \tau}{\optS\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{the}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\optS\tau}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\theS t}{\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{pair}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\tau} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\sigma}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\pairS{x}{y}}{\tau \timesS \sigma}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{fst}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau \timesS \sigma}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\fstS t}{\tau}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{snd}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau \timesS \sigma}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{\sndS t}{\sigma}}
  \end{mathpar}
  \paragraph{Arithmetic.}
  \begin{mathpar}
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{le}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\intS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\intS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \leS y}{\boolS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{add}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\intS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\intS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \addS y}{\intS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{sub}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\intS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\intS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \subS y}{\intS}}
    \and
    \typingrule{smt-concrete-typing}{mul}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x}{\intS} \quad \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\intS}}
      {\stype{\Gamma}{x \mulS y}{\intS}}
  \end{mathpar}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:smt-lambda-uncurried}
  The SMT type \(\tau^{\times} \toS \gamma\) assigned to an \(n\)-ary abstraction
  is \emph{uncurried}: the binder is typed as a unary function whose domain is the
  right-nested product \(\tau^{\times}\) of its argument SMT types. This is a
  deliberate departure from SMT-LIB, whose own treatment of \(\lambda\)-abstraction
  is the classical, curried discipline of higher-order logic: the multi-ary form
  \(\mathtt{lambda}\ ((x_1 : \tau_1)\, \cdots\, (x_n : \tau_n))\ t\) abbreviates the nesting
  of unary abstractions
  \(\mathtt{lambda}\ (x_1 : \tau_1)\ \bigl(\cdots \bigl(\mathtt{lambda}\ (x_n : \tau_n)\ t\bigr) \cdots\bigr)\),
  sorted with the right-associative binary arrow as
  \(\tau_1 \toS \cdots \toS \tau_n \toS \gamma\).

  We adopt the uncurried, tuple-based convention instead. It is the natural
  mathematical reading of a binder---a function of several variables \emph{is} a
  function of one tuple-valued variable; it keeps application total, since a
  function is always applied to a single tupled argument and never partially; and
  it aligns the SMT image of a B binder with the tuple encoding used pervasively in
  \Cref{ch:beer}. No expressiveness is lost, the two presentations being related by
  the standard currying isomorphism
  \((\tau_1 \timesS \cdots \timesS \tau_n) \toS \gamma \,\cong\, \tau_1 \toS \cdots \toS \tau_n \toS \gamma\).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Properties of the concrete type system}

We establish the structural properties of the concrete type system that are used
in the correctness arguments.

\paragraph{Rule inversion.}
As on the source side, we prove one inversion lemma per typing rule, allowing a
typing derivation to be decomposed into the premises of the unique applicable
rule. They are available under the names
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.Typing.appE}{SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.appE}},
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.Typing.pairE}{SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.pairE}},
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.Typing.lambdaE}{SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.lambdaE}},
\etc, and each proof is immediate by case analysis on the derivation, which
reduces to a single branch. For instance, inversion for pairs reads as follows.

\begin{lemma}[Pair typing inversion][SMT.Typing.pairE][SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.pairE]
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a typing context, \(x, y\) concrete SMT terms, and \(\tau\)
  an SMT type. If \(\stype{\Gamma}{\pairS(x, y)}{\tau}\), then \(\tau\) is a pair
  SMT type whose components type the two projections:
  \[
    \exists\,\alpha,\beta,\quad \tau = \alpha \timesS \beta \;\land\;
    \stype{\Gamma}{x}{\alpha} \;\land\; \stype{\Gamma}{y}{\beta}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Bound variables and the context.}
A basic structural invariant is that the bound variables of a well-typed term are
disjoint from the typing context. It is the engine behind the freshness side
conditions of the binder rules and is used repeatedly in the abstraction
arguments below.

\begin{lemma}[Bound variables are fresh][SMT.Typing.bv\_disjoint][SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.bv_disjoint]
  \label{lem:smt-bv-disjoint}
  If \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\), then \(\mathsf{bv}(t)\) is disjoint from the
  keys of \(\Gamma\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the typing derivation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Strengthening.}
Typing depends only on the bindings of the variables that actually occur free in
a term; bindings for variables absent from the term may be discarded.

\begin{theorem}[Strengthening][SMT.Typing.strengthening\_of\_fv\_subset][SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.strengthening_of_fv_subset]
  \label{thm:smt-strengthening}
  Let \(\Gamma, \Xi\) be typing contexts with
  \(\Gamma \subseteq \Xi\), let \(t\) be a concrete SMT term, and let
  \(\tau\) be an SMT type. If \(\stype{\Xi}{t}{\tau}\) and every free
  variable of \(t\) is bound in \(\Gamma\), then
  \[
    \stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}.
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the derivation \(\stype{\Xi}{t}{\tau}\).
  
  In the variable case \(t = \mathsf{var}\ v\),
  the hypothesis gives \(v \in \Gamma\); since
  \(\Gamma \subseteq \Xi\) and \(\Xi(v) = \tau\), the two contexts
  agree on \(v\), and the variable rule applies under \(\Gamma\).
  
  Constants are immediate; compound non-binder terms follow from the induction
  hypotheses on their subterms.
  
  For a binder, the induction hypothesis is applied to the body under the contexts extended by the
  bound variables, \(\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau] \subseteq \Xi[\vec{v}:\vec\tau]\); a free variable of the body is either bound by the binder---and
  hence present in the extension---or free in the whole term, and thus in
  \(\Gamma\). The freshness side condition \(\mathsf{Bind}\) is preserved because the
  bound variables, fresh for \(\Xi\), remain fresh for the smaller context \(\Gamma\);
  its remaining conjuncts concern only the bound variables and are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weakening][SMT.Typing.weakening][SMT/Typing.html\#SMT.Typing.weakening]
  \label{thm:smt-weakening}
  Let \(\Gamma, \Xi\) be typing contexts with
  \(\Gamma \subseteq \Xi\), let \(t\) be a concrete SMT term, and let
  \(\tau\) be an SMT type. If \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\) and no bound variable of
  \(t\) occurs in \(\Xi\)---equivalently, in \(\Xi \setminus \Gamma\), since
  \(\mathsf{bv}(t)\) is already disjoint from \(\Gamma\) by
  \Cref{lem:smt-bv-disjoint}---then
  \[
    \stype{\Xi}{t}{\tau}.
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the derivation \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\); the non-binder cases are
  immediate.
  
  In a binder case, the freshness premise of \(\mathsf{Bind}\) \eqref{eq:def-bind-smt} under \(\Xi\)
  is exactly the added hypothesis, and it propagates to the body, whose bound variables
  remain fresh for the extension \(\Xi[\vec v:\vec\tau]\): they avoid \(\Xi\) by
  hypothesis and avoid the binder's own variables by \(\mathsf{Bind}\). This is the sole
  point of departure from strengthening: enlarging the context can violate binder
  freshness, whence the side condition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Abstract typing rules}
\label{subsec:smt-abstract-typing}

Abstract typing is defined over the PHOAS syntax. Abstract typing contexts are again
partial functions \(\Gamma : \mathcal{V} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{SMTType}\) where
\(\mathcal{V}\) is a parameterized variable type.
Abstract judgments are written \(\satype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\) and are available as the
inductive predicate {\small\leancodeptr{SMT.PHOAS.Typing}{SMT/HOTyping/Rules.html\#SMT.PHOAS.Typing}}.

The non-binder rules are identical to their concrete counterparts. As in
\Cref{subsec:b-abstract-typing}, the binder rules differ in how they expose the
bound variables: variable management is delegated to Lean's meta-level, and the
body is typed under any extension of the context with arbitrary families of
variables carrying the prescribed SMT type---in practice, \(\mathcal{V}\) is equipped
with an intrinsic type-assignment function, mirroring the the constraint enforced on
the source side.

We illustrate the binder rules with the lambda rule; the universal rule is
analogous. Given a family of SMT types \(\vec\tau : \mathsf{Fin}\,n \to
\mathsf{SMTType}\) with \(n > 0\), an SMT type \(\gamma\), and a scope 
\(e : (\mathsf{Fin}\,n \to \mathcal{V}) \to \mathsf{Term}\,\mathcal{V}\):

\begin{mathpar}
  \typingrule{smt-abstract-typing}{lambda}
  {\forall \vec v,\, \satype{\Gamma[\vec v:\vec\tau]}{e(\vec v)}{\gamma}}
  {\satype{\Gamma}{\lamS \vec\tau \cdot e}{\tau^{\times} \toS \gamma}}
\end{mathpar}
where the vector \(\vec v\) of variables ranges over all families of variables carrying the
expected SMT types.

As for the concrete system, we prove inversion lemmas for every abstract rule,
with the same names as their concrete counterparts, \eg\@
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.PHOAS.Typing.appE}{SMT/HOTyping/Rules.html\#SMT.PHOAS.Typing.appE}},
{\small\leancodeptr{SMT.PHOAS.Typing.lambdaE}{SMT/HOTyping/Rules.html\#SMT.PHOAS.Typing.lambdaE}},
\etc

\paragraph{Determinism.}
A basic expected property of the abstract type system is determinism: a term has at most
one SMT type in a given context. It is the type-level invariant on which the semantic
adequacy result of \Cref{thm:smt-denote-type-det} rests.

\begin{theorem}[Typing determinism][SMT.PHOAS.Typing.det][SMT/HOTyping/Rules.html\#SMT.PHOAS.Typing.det]
  \label{thm:smt-typing-det}
  Let \(\Gamma\) be an abstract typing context, \(t\) an abstract SMT term, and
  \(\tau, \sigma\) SMT types. If \(\satype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\) and
  \(\satype{\Gamma}{t}{\sigma}\), then \(\tau = \sigma\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on \(t\). Since the same unique inversion rule applies to both derivations in
  each case, the result follows from the induction hypothesis and the injectivity of type
  constructors. In a binder case, the induction hypothesis is applied to the body
  instantiated at a fixed family of variables carrying the bound sorts---which exists
  because the variable type provides a representative of every sort---thereby discharging
  the quantification over admissible variable families.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Abstracting concrete typing}
\label{subsec:smt-typing-abstraction}

The two type systems are connected by a context abstraction that lifts concrete
typing derivations to the PHOAS setting. As in \Cref{subsec:b-typing-abstraction},
the variable type \(\mathcal{V}\) is, in practice, instantiated with a semantic domain satisfying
the constraint mentioned earlier.

Recall that a renaming, or valuation when instantiated with the semantic domain, is a partial map
\(\Delta : \mathsf{String} \rightharpoonup \mathcal{V}\). We first abstract typing contexts.

\begin{definition}[Context abstraction][SMT.TypeContext.abstract][SMT/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#SMT.TypeContext.abstract]
  \label{def:smt-context-abstraction}
      Let \(\Gamma\) be a concrete typing context and
  \(\Delta : \mathsf{String} \rightharpoonup \mathcal{V}\) a renaming. The
  abstract context \(\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta} : \mathcal{V} \rightharpoonup
  \mathsf{SMTType}\) is defined on \(v : \mathcal{V}\) whenever there is a
  concrete name \(k\) with \(\Delta(k) = v\) and \(\Gamma(k) = \tau\), where
  \(\tau\) is the intrinsic SMT type carried by \(v\); in that case it maps \(v\) to
  \(\tau\):
  \[
    \aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}(v) \coloneq
      \tau \quad\text{if } \exists k,\, \Delta(k) = v \land \Gamma(k) = \tau
  \]
\end{definition}

The coherence condition \(\Gamma(k) = \tau\) requires the SMT type declared by
\(\Gamma\) for the preimage \(k\) to coincide with the intrinsic SMT type of the
domain element \(v\). Baking this check into the definition makes
\(\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}\) well-formed by construction, in the following sense.

\begin{definition}[Well-formed abstract context][SMT.PHOAS.WFTC][SMT/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#SMT.PHOAS.WFTC]
  \label{def:smt-wftc}
  An abstract context \(\Gamma : \mathcal{V} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{SMTType}\)
  is \emph{well-formed} if every assignment respects the intrinsic SMT type:
  for all \(v\) and \(\tau\), if \(\Gamma(v) = \tau\) then \(v\) carries the intrinsic SMT
  type \(\tau\) in the domain \(\mathcal{V}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Abstraction is well-formed][SMT.PHOAS.WFTC.of\_abstract][SMT/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#SMT.PHOAS.WFTC.of_abstract]
  \label{lem:smt-wftc-of-abstract}
  For any renaming \(\Delta\) and concrete context \(\Gamma\), the abstract
  context \(\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}\) is well-formed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Follows immediately from \Cref{def:smt-context-abstraction}.
\end{proof}

The main result is that concrete typing is preserved by abstraction, mirroring
\Cref{thm:b-typing-of-abstract}.
As on the source side, it requires the renaming to cover the free variables and to be
SMT type-compatible with \(\Gamma\).

\begin{theorem}[\(\,\sop{\vdash} \implies \saop{\vdash}\,\)][SMT.Typing.of\_abstract][SMT/Reasoning/Lemmas.html\#SMT.Typing.of_abstract]
  \label{thm:smt-typing-of-abstract}
  Let \(\Gamma\) be a concrete typing context, \(t\) a concrete SMT term,
  \(\tau\) an SMT type, and \(\Delta\) a renaming such that
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), nosep]
    \item \(\Delta\) is defined on every free variable of \(t\), and
    \item \(\Delta\) is SMT type-compatible with \(\Gamma\): whenever
          \(\Gamma(v) = \sigma\), the value \(\Delta(v)\) is defined and carries
          intrinsic SMT type \(\sigma\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Then typing is preserved by abstraction:
  \[
    \stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau} \implies \satype{\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}}{\aterm{t}{\Delta}}{\tau}.
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By induction on the derivation \(\stype{\Gamma}{t}{\tau}\).

  In the variable case \(t = \mathsf{var}\ v\), inversion gives
  \(\Gamma(v) = \tau\); SMT type-compatibility yields a domain value
  \(\Delta(v)\) of intrinsic SMT type \(\tau\), and by
  \Cref{def:smt-context-abstraction} the abstract context maps it to
  \(\aterm{\Gamma}{\Delta}(v) \triangleq \Gamma(v) = \tau\). Since
  \(\aterm{\mathsf{var}\ v}{\Delta} \triangleq \Delta(v)\), the abstract variable rule
  concludes. Constants carry no free variables and are immediate.

  For compound non-binders, combining rule inversion with induction hypotheses
  to each subterm (the renaming still covers their free variables), and the corresponding abstract
  rule reconstructs the derivation.

  For binders, both the context and the renaming are extended with fresh bindings
  for the bound variables: a family \(\vec v\) of domain values carrying the
  SMT types \(\vec\tau\) is introduced, the renaming is updated, and the induction hypothesis is
  invoked on the body under the extended context.
\end{proof}
