\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:beer-discussion}

In \emph{Reflections on Trusting Trust}, his Turing Award lecture published in 1984, Ken Thompson showed how a compromised compiler could insert a Trojan horse into generated programs and perpetuate the attack through recompilation even after the malicious code had been removed from the compiler's source~\cite{thompson1984reflections}.
He drew the following conclusion:
\begin{quotation}[Ken Thompson][1984][Reflections on Trusting Trust]
  No amount of source-level verification or scrutiny will protect you from using untrusted code.
\end{quotation}
Applied to formal verification, Thompson's warning translates into a simple principle: a verification result is only as strong as what it assumes.
We therefore close the chapter by listing exactly what a user of \beer{} trusts beyond the proofs of \Cref{sec:beer-soundness}.

\subsection{Trusted computing base}
\label{sec:beer-tcb}

First, the generic infrastructure: the Lean~4 kernel and the consistency of Mathlib, and the ZFC model of \Cref{ch:zflean} on which every denotation of this thesis is built.
Second, the \emph{decoder}: the parser translating Atelier~B proof obligation files into the B terms of \Cref{ch:b} is unverified, and so are the elaborations it performs---rewriting \(\subseteqB\) into a bounded universal and the function spaces into comprehensions over \(\pfunB\).
An error there changes \emph{which} statement is proved, not whether the proof is correct.
Third, the \emph{back-end}: the printer from the abstract script to concrete SMT-LIB syntax, and the SMT solver itself, trusted to answer \texttt{unsat} only for scripts unsatisfiable in the semantics of \Cref{ch:smt}; assembly of the script-level theorem from the mechanized term contracts is likewise a paper argument (\Cref{sec:beer-po-soundness}).

Finally, the formal development has an explicit axiom audit.
Lean has a command \leaninl{#print axioms}, which reports that both {\small\leancodeptr{encodeTerm\_rep\_spec}{SMT/Reasoning/EncodeTermRepresented.html\#encodeTerm_rep_spec}} and its Boolean corollary rely only on the usual logical principles admitted by Lean: propositional extensionality, classical choice, and quotient soundness.
In particular, the representation-aware proof introduces no project-specific axiom or proof placeholder: the represented universal and its declaration-aware helper contract are assembled inside the proved structural induction rather than assumed at its boundary.

This development nevertheless has two limitations.
The first concerns unsupported constructs.
The encoder rejects \(\cardB{\cdot}\), \(\minB\), \(\maxB\), and other constructs like operations on sequences: no encoding rule covers them, and the encoder fails. \Cref{thm:beer-encodeTerm-spec} constrains successful runs only.
This failure mode is the benign one: a proof obligation containing an unsupported construct is never translated, so no unsound script is ever produced; the cost is coverage, not correctness.

The second limitation is the representation blow-up caused by loosening.
Each cast introduces a helper variable and a quantified specification, and the \encruleref{rule:beer-bounded-universal} rule nests these under the source quantifier, adding quantifier alternations to the emitted formula.
Characteristic predicate representations likewise trade solver-friendly function values for higher-order terms; this is the ``encoding-functions-as-functions'' principle that ruled the design choices advocated in \Cref{ch:loosening}.
The practical impact of both on solver performance is measured in \Cref{ch:evaluation}.

\subsection{Zero-Proof \beer{}}
\label{sec:beer-zero-proof}

The limitations above concern the fragment for which the encoder and its soundness proof are maintained in lockstep.
This coupling is valuable for certification, but makes every extension of the source language or optimization of the encoder a proof-engineering task as well as an implementation task.
For uses that favor coverage and translation speed over certification, we therefore also maintain \emph{\textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{}}\footnote{The name plays on the two senses of \emph{proof}: zero-proof drinks are alcohol-free, while \textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{} omits the formal proof of correctness.}, a variant from which the correctness development is deliberately elided.
Freed from the requirement that every implementation change preserve the proof architecture, it supports a larger fragment of B and can be optimized more aggressively.

The variant drops the mechanized layer entirely: the PHOAS and denotational developments, the reasoning modules behind \Cref{sec:beer-soundness}, and, inside the encoder itself, the machinery whose only consumer was a proof---the dependent in-domain test guarding each context lookup, and the copy of~$\Gamma$ taken on entering a binder so that a lemma may relate the two.
Coverage then extends to constructs the verified fragment leaves out: \(\cardB{\cdot}\), \(\minB\), \(\maxB\), finiteness, integer division and exponentiation, transitive closure, the sequence operators, generalized sum and product, and the parallel product.
The rest is performance work on both sides of the interface.
Inside the encoder, efficient data structures like hash-maps are used, and states are updated linearly instead of cloned.

\textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{} therefore offers no mechanized semantic link between a source obligation and the SMT script it emits, and the verified encoder remains the reference for the guarantees established here.
The two share a design and differ in what they are built for.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:beer-conclusion}

This chapter has established a translation from B terms to SMT terms and its correctness in a representation-aware semantics.
Its central invariant relates source denotations not to a single canonical target type, but to every representation that the encoder may select and to the exact loosening paths between them.
The resulting soundness theorem covers the complete successful execution of the term encoder.
Together with the argument of \Cref{sec:beer-po-soundness}, it explains why an unsatisfiable generated script is evidence for the validity of the source obligation, subject precisely to the trusted components identified above.
