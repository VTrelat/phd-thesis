\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:beer-discussion}

\subsection{Trusted computing base}
\label{sec:beer-tcb}

A verification result is only as strong as what it assumes; we close the chapter by listing exactly what a user of \beer{} trusts beyond the proofs of \Cref{sec:beer-soundness}.

First, the generic infrastructure: the Lean~4 kernel and the consistency of Mathlib, and the ZFC model of \Cref{ch:zflean} on which every denotation of this thesis is built.
Second, the \emph{decoder}: the parser translating Atelier~B proof-obligation files into the B terms of \Cref{ch:b} is unverified, and so are the elaborations it performs---rewriting \(\subseteqB\) into a bounded universal, the function spaces into comprehensions over \(\pfunB\), and the detection of flagged variables (\Cref{sec:beer-rules-cast}).
An error there changes \emph{which} statement is proved, not whether the proof is correct.
Third, the \emph{back end}: the printer from the abstract script to concrete SMT-LIB syntax, and the SMT solver itself, trusted to answer \textsf{unsat} only for scripts unsatisfiable in the semantics of \Cref{ch:smt}; the assembly of \Cref{thm:beer-end-to-end} from the mechanized \Cref{thm:beer-encodeTerm-spec} is likewise a paper argument (\Cref{sec:beer-po-soundness}).

Finally, the residual assumptions inside the development itself, all localized and \texttt{axiom}-tagged ({\small\leancodeptr{Axioms}{SMT/Reasoning/Axioms.html}}, {\small\leancodeptr{AllCaseHelpers}{SMT/Reasoning/Basic/AllCaseHelpers.html}}).
They fall into three groups.
Five are variable-scoping facts---the encoder only binds names drawn from its monotone fresh supply, so generated binders never capture source variables---which are operationally evident from the encoder's definition and provable by (large, tedious) structural inductions that were not carried out.
One asserts the well-typedness of the emitted guarded universal of \cref{eq:beer-forall-shape}, again a structural induction.
One admits a weakest-precondition subcase of the universal quantifier over flagged binders on an empty domain.
To these one must add the two witness hypotheses threaded by \Cref{thm:beer-encodeTerm-spec} for the quantifier case (\Cref{sec:beer-case-forall}): the underlying infimum computations are mechanized as standalone lemmas, but their wiring into the induction is assumed rather than proved.
Everything else---the bridge, the encoding rules, and every case argument of \Cref{sec:beer-soundness}---is proved from first principles.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:beer-limitations}

\paragraph{Unsupported constructs.}
The encoder rejects \(\cardB{\cdot}\): the constructor is declared in the term language, but no encoding rule exists and the encoder fails on it, so the corresponding induction case of \Cref{thm:beer-encodeTerm-spec} holds vacuously---the theorem constrains only successful runs.
The same holds for the minimum and maximum operators, which the Lean term language carries but which lie outside the fragment presented in \Cref{ch:b}.
This failure mode is the benign one: a proof obligation containing an unsupported construct is never translated, so no unsound script is ever produced; the cost is coverage, quantified in \Cref{ch:evaluation}.

\paragraph{Partially verified cast paths.}
As stated in \Cref{sec:beer-case-pointwise}, the denotational theorems for the binary set operations cover their homogeneous branches, which are the ones reachable when every variable has its canonical type.
The heterogeneous branches---the graph-path union of the running example among them---are verified for typing, totality, and state hygiene, and the loosened-membership adequacy that the quantifier case consumes is proved \(\Delta\)-universally; but their full denotational specifications are not yet wired into the main induction, and quantification over \emph{flagged} binders additionally relies on the witness hypotheses discussed above.
The mathematics required is loosening correctness (\Cref{thm:loosening-correctness}), already available; the gap is proof engineering, not theory.

\paragraph{Representation blow-up.}
Loosening is not free.
Each cast introduces a helper variable and a quantified specification, and the guarded-universal scoping of \cref{eq:beer-forall-shape} nests these under the source quantifier, adding quantifier alternations to the emitted formula.
Characteristic-predicate representations likewise trade solver-friendly function values for higher-order terms.
The practical impact of both on solver performance is deliberately out of scope here and is measured in \Cref{ch:evaluation}.

\subsection{Outlook}
\label{sec:beer-outlook}

The path forward is largely mapped by the previous subsection: discharge the scoping axioms by the structural inductions they call for, wire the existing cast-path specifications into the induction to cover heterogeneous operations and flagged binders without hypotheses, and mechanize the script-level assembly of \Cref{thm:beer-end-to-end}.
On the encoding side, \(\cardB{\cdot}\) admits a finite-cardinality axiomatization, and tighter representations---keeping both operands functional when possible instead of loosening to graphs---would relieve the solver where \Cref{ch:evaluation} shows it struggling.
None of these steps requires new mathematics: the bridge of \Cref{sec:beer-semantic-bridge} and the loosening theory of \Cref{ch:loosening} were designed to carry them.
