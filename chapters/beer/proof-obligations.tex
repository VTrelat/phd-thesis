\section{Encoding proof obligations}
\label{sec:beer-proof-obligations}

Term soundness is the kernel: this section explains the corresponding end-to-end result for proof obligations.
A decoded proof obligation (\Cref{ch:b}) consists of a typing context, a list of definitions and hypotheses, and one or more goals.
The {\small\leancodeptr{encode}{Encoder/Encoder.html\#encode}} function processes a whole B environment by encoding each proof obligation in turn, each stage emitting declarations or assertions into the growing SMT script.

\subsection{From terms to environments}
\label{sec:beer-po-environments}

The typing context is emitted first.
For every B binding \(v:\alpha\), the context encoder selects either the canonical type \(\toSMT{\alpha}\), or, when the binding is selected as a binary function, the option-valued type \(\toSMT{\beta}\toS\optS\,\toSMT{\gamma}\) for \(\alpha=\setB(\beta\prodB\gamma)\).
The resulting pair of contexts satisfies the \emph{representation-context} invariant: the type of every target binding lies in the adjoint interval associated with the canonical encoding of its source type.
Named definitions are recorded through the SMT-LIB command \smtinl{define-fun} over their encoded bodies, and the environment's distinctness and finiteness constraints are encoded and asserted.
Each proof obligation carries the list of definition blocks it may use, and only those are asserted, locally.
A final pass declares the residual variables that were typed during encoding but not yet declared---bound variables of constraints whose helper specifications were emitted globally.

\subsection{Refutation and semantic adequacy}
\label{sec:beer-po-refutation}
\label{sec:beer-po-soundness}

Each proof obligation is encoded and scoped inside its own level using \smtinl{(push 1) ... (pop 1)} blocks containing their local declarations, asserted definitions and hypotheses, and then, per goal, a nested block asserting the \emph{negated} encoded goal followed by \smtinl{check-sat}.
The scoping is not cosmetic.
Atelier~B synthesizes fresh identifiers per obligation and freely reuses a name at \emph{different types} across obligations; the local blocks realize the corresponding scoping discipline on the SMT side, so that each \smtinl{check-sat} sees exactly the declarations and assertions of its own obligation.
One script thus carries one \smtinl{check-sat} per goal.
Each answer is interpreted independently: \texttt{unsat} discharges that goal, \texttt{sat} exhibits a countermodel and therefore invalidates it, while \texttt{unknown} or a timeout leaves it open.

Consider one proof obligation
\[
  H_1,\cdots,H_n\ \vdash\ G
\]
over a B typing context \(\Gamma_{\mathsf B}\).
Its meaning is direct: it is valid when every valuation \(\Delta\), well formed for \(\Gamma_{\mathsf B}\) and defined on the free variables of the obligation, that makes all the hypotheses true also makes the goal true.
Equivalently, invalidity is witnessed by a B countermodel satisfying
\[
  \interpB{\aterm{H_i}{\Delta}}=\denval{\topZ}[\boolB]
  \quad\text{for every \(i\)},\qquad
  \interpB{\aterm{G}{\Delta}}=\denval{\botZ}[\boolB].
\]
Hypotheses and goals are assumed well-defined, consistently with Atelier~B's separate emission of well-definedness obligations (\Cref{sec:b-wd}).

Assume that the obligation is well typed and its encoding succeeds.
If all function-selected free variables are justified by true, well-typed function-space hypotheses and bounded universals satisfy binder compatibility (\Cref{fig:beer-binder-compatibility}), then the refutational reduction is preserved by encoding:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    H_1,\cdots,H_n\vdash G
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    H_1,\cdots,H_n,\notB G\vdash\bot\\
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \text{the emitted refutation block is unsatisfiable}.
  \end{aligned}
\]
At a fixed SMT scope, all active \smtinl{assert} commands are conjoined.
The nested goal block therefore asks whether
\[
  H_1' \andS \cdots \andS H_n' \andS \notS G'
\]
has a model, together with the surrounding local assertions; this is the refutational computation performed by \smtinl{check-sat}.

The proof uses the equivalent countermodel correspondence shown in \Cref{fig:beer-refutation-adequacy}.
Retracting an SMT model yields an agreeing B valuation, with option-valued functions read as graphs; the Boolean term corollary \Cref{cor:beer-encodeTerm-bool-spec} then recovers true B hypotheses and a false goal.
Conversely, represent a B countermodel at the selected SMT types and interpret each generated helper by its specification; the same corollary makes every encoded hypothesis and the negated goal true.

The valuation transports and term-level results are mechanized; their assembly over all assertions and scopes is not.
Interpreting solver answers also assumes solver soundness for the ZF semantics of \Cref{ch:smt} as discussed later in \Cref{sec:beer-tcb}.

\subsection{The running example}
\label{sec:beer-po-running-example}

We follow the encoding of \cref{eq:beer-running-po} to the final solver call using fragments of the complete input in \Cref{lst:beer-running-po}.
They omit unused machinery and administrative scopes and simplify fresh names and definitions using the datatype equations.
The negated universal is retained before final propositional simplification to expose the refutational structure; these changes preserve the active SMT formula.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Global prelude.}
Before encoding individual proof obligations, the encoder emits the shared datatype declarations as a global prelude, shown in \Cref{lst:beer-running-prelude}, outside every proof-obligation scope.

\begin{smtlisting}[title={Global datatype prelude.}, label=lst:beer-running-prelude, float=h]
(declare-datatype Pair (par (T1 T2) ((pair (fst T1) (snd T2)))))
(declare-datatype Option (par (T) ((some (the T)) (none))))
\end{smtlisting}

\paragraph{Selecting the representations.}
Sets of integers use their canonical characteristic predicate representation from \Cref{def:beer-type-encoding}.
The context instead selects the functional representation for \(\var{f}\), justified by the hypothesis \(\var{f}\inB X\tfunB Y\), while \(\var{g}\) remains relational.
The encoder then increments the current scope level with \smtinl{(push 1)}.
Within that level, the declarations in \Cref{lst:beer-running-declarations} assign the two operands of the union different SMT types.

\begin{smtlisting}[title={Selected representations.}, label=lst:beer-running-declarations, float=h]
(declare-const X (-> Int Bool))
(declare-const Y (-> Int Bool))
(declare-const f (-> Int (Option Int)))
(declare-const g (-> (Pair Int Int) Bool))
\end{smtlisting}

\paragraph{Encoding the assumptions.}
After elaboration of the derived function and relation spaces, the \encruleref{rule:beer-membership}, \encruleref{rule:beer-partial-function-space}, \encruleref{rule:beer-powerset}, and \encruleref{rule:beer-cartesian-product} rules yield the two assertions in \Cref{lst:beer-running-assumptions}.
For \(\var{f}\), the first conjunct confines its graph to \(X\prodB Y\), and the second makes its domain total on \(X\); single-valuedness is already enforced by the option-valued function representation.
The assertion for \(\var{g}\) states directly that every pair in its graph belongs to \(X\prodB Y\).

\begin{smtlisting}[title={Encoded assumptions.}, label=lst:beer-running-assumptions, float=h]
(assert
  (and
    (forall ((u Int) (v Int))
      (=> (= (f u) (some v)) (and (X u) (Y v))))
    (forall ((u Int))
      (=> (X u) (exists ((v Int)) (= (f u) (some v)))))))
(assert
  (forall ((p (Pair Int Int)))
    (=> (g p)
      (exists ((u Int) (v Int))
        (and (X u) (Y v) (= p (pair u v)))))))
\end{smtlisting}

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Loosening inside the goal.}
The union cannot combine the option-valued \(\var{f}\) with the relational \(\var{g}\) directly.
Following \Cref{sec:beer-rules-cast}, the \encruleref{rule:beer-union} rule keeps \(\var{g}\) and loosens \(\var{f}\) to the fresh graph predicate \smtinl{f!} specified in \Cref{lst:beer-running-loosening}.

\begin{smtlisting}[title={Loosening to a graph.}, label=lst:beer-running-loosening, float=h]
(declare-const f! (-> (Pair Int Int) Bool))
(define-fun f!_spec () Bool
  (forall ((u Int) (v Int))
    (= (f! (pair u v)) (= (f u) (some v)))))
(assert f!_spec)
\end{smtlisting}

The specification says that \smtinl{(pair u v)} belongs to \smtinl{f!} exactly when \smtinl{f} returns \smtinl{(some v)} at \smtinl{u}.
After this reconciliation, union membership reduces to \smtinl{(or (f! z) (g z))}.
The specification depends only on the free variable \smtinl{f}, not on the quantified pair \smtinl{z}.
The helper can therefore be declared outside the quantified goal, with its specification asserted in the proof-obligation scope.
The re-scoping part of the \encruleref{rule:beer-bounded-universal} rule is needed only for helpers whose specifications depend on a bound variable; in this instance, the encoded goal quantifies \smtinl{z} alone.
Its guard is the union predicate above, and its existential conclusion is the characteristic predicate produced for \(X\prodB Y\) by the \encruleref{rule:beer-cartesian-product} rule.

\paragraph{Refutation.}
The proof obligation layer leaves the assumptions in the outer scope and asserts the negation of the encoded bounded universal in a nested goal scope.
The resulting block in \Cref{lst:beer-running-refutation} makes the final step explicit: its active assertions express exactly the two hypotheses together with the negation of the goal, as explained in \Cref{sec:beer-po-refutation}.

\begin{smtlisting}[title={Negated goal.}, label=lst:beer-running-refutation, float=h]
(push 1)
  (assert
    (not
      (forall ((z (Pair Int Int)))
        (=> (or (f! z) (g z))
          (exists ((u Int) (v Int))
            (and (X u) (Y v) (= z (pair u v))))))))
  (check-sat)
(pop 1)
\end{smtlisting}

The complete emitted script is given in \Cref{lst:beer-running-po}.
Running cvc5 (\Cref{sec:ar-smt}) with the following options returns \textsf{unsat} on this script:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{incremental} to enable incremental solving (\smtinl{push} and \smtinl{pop}) even if this script contains only one \smtinl{check-sat};
  \item \texttt{mbqi} to enable model-based quantifier instantiation for higher-order quantifiers.
\end{itemize}
Indeed, the assumptions place both graphs in \(X\prodB Y\), and the loosening specification preserves the graph of \(\var{f}\), so their union cannot contain a counterexample to the inclusion.
By the refutational criterion, \cref{eq:beer-running-po} is valid.
