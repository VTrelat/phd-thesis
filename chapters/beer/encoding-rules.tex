\section{Encoding Rules}
\label{sec:beer-encoding-rules}

This section presents the term encoder as a partial, syntax-directed translation from B terms to SMT terms.
Most rules follow the structure of the source term: base values and elementary operators translate directly, sets become characteristic predicates, and lambdas become their graphs.
The main difficulty is that a single B type may admit several SMT representations.
Each recursive call therefore returns not only an SMT term but also its actual SMT type; when encoded subterms meet, the cast layer reconciles their representations along the loosening paths of \Cref{ch:loosening}.

We first introduce the encoding judgment and the elementary rules, then treat set constructors, comprehensions, and lambdas.
We next isolate the casts required by set operations, equality, membership, and application, before explaining how generated helpers and their specifications are scoped under bounded universal quantifiers.
Together, these rules describe the mathematical behavior of the encoder whose correctness is established in \Cref{sec:beer-soundness}.
For each construct, we state the recursive encodings of its subterms first, display the resulting encoding rule in a box, and then give its intuition.

\subsection{The encoding judgment}
\label{sec:beer-encoding-judgment}

The translation is partial: it can fail on unsupported constructs or when operand representations cannot be reconciled, in which case no SMT term is produced. The correctness result therefore concerns successful encodings of well-typed, well-defined inputs that fall within the supported fragment defined in \Cref{ch:b}.
The encoding judgment is relative to a local B typing context
\(\bop{\Gamma} : \mathsf{String} \rightharpoonup \mathsf{BType}\), as defined in \Cref{subsec:b-concrete-typing}.
This context is produced on the fly by the decoder when reading a proof obligation file generated by Atelier~B: each identifier in the proof-obligation refers to an entry in its type table, and the resulting bindings between variables and their types form a file-wide context; bindings local to the proof obligation being encoded are added to that context.
The encoder then derives from \(\bop{\Gamma}\) an ambient SMT typing context \(\sop{\Gamma}\), recording for each variable of B type \(\alpha\) an SMT representation in the interval
\(\bigl[\lgf{(\toSMT{\alpha})},\mgf{(\toSMT{\alpha})}\bigr]\) of \Cref{ch:loosening}, as introduced in \Cref{sec:beer-type-encoding}.
Given a concrete B term \(t\) such that \(\btype{\bop{\Gamma}}{t}{\alpha}\), when the encoder succeeds and returns the concrete SMT term \(t'\) together with an SMT type \(\sigma\), we write
\[
  t \encto (t', \sigma).
\]
The contexts are implicit in this notation.
The source type \(\alpha\) is also omitted because it is a typing property of \(t\), whereas \(\sigma\) is computed and returned by the encoder. \Cref{thm:beer-encodeTerm-spec} proves, in particular, that \(\sigma\) is such that
\[
  \stype{\sop{\Gamma}}{t'}{\sigma} \;\;\land\;\; \castable{\sigma}{\toSMT{\alpha}}.
\]

\subsection{Atoms, arithmetic, logic, and pairs}
\label{sec:beer-rules-homomorphic}

The base fragment is homomorphic: each B operator maps to its SMT counterpart, with operands encoded recursively.

The atomic terms require no recursive call.
\begin{encrule}[atoms]{rule:beer-atoms}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \var{v} &\encto (\var{v},\, \sop{\Gamma}(\var{v}))\mkern20mu
  & n &\encto (n,\, \intS)\mkern20mu
  & b &\encto (b,\, \boolS)
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
A variable keeps its name and receives the representation recorded in the SMT context; integer and Boolean literals keep both their syntax and their base type.

Suppose \(x \encto (x', \intS)\) and \(y \encto (y', \intS)\).
\begin{encrule}[arithmetic]{rule:beer-arithmetic}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  x \addB y &\encto (x' \addS y',\, \intS)\mkern20mu
  & x \subB y &\encto (x' \subS y',\, \intS)\mkern20mu
  & x \mulB y &\encto (x' \mulS y',\, \intS) \\
  x \leB y &\encto (x' \leS y',\, \boolS)
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
Arithmetic is translated homomorphically, with comparisons returning a Boolean.

Suppose \(x \encto (x', \boolS)\) and \(y \encto (y', \boolS)\).
\begin{encrule}[logic]{rule:beer-logic}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  x \andB y &\encto (x' \andS y',\, \boolS)\mkern20mu
  & \notB x &\encto (\notS x',\, \boolS)
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
The primitive connectives are translated directly.
No separate rules are needed for \(\orB\), \(\impB\), or \(\iffB\): as noted in \Cref{ch:b}, they are generated from \(\andB\) and \(\notB\) and are eliminated before encoding.

Suppose \(x \encto (x', \sigma_x)\) and \(y \encto (y', \sigma_y)\).
\begin{encrule}[maplet]{rule:beer-maplet}
\[
  x \mapletB y \encto (\pairS{x'}{y'},\, \sigma_x \timesS \sigma_y)
\]
\end{encrule}
A B maplet becomes an SMT pair whose components retain the representations selected by their recursive encodings.

\subsection{Sets as characteristic predicates}
\label{sec:beer-rules-sets}

Following \Cref{def:beer-type-encoding}, the encoder constructs sets as characteristic predicates.
The predicate domain is the representation produced for the element type: if elements are encoded at \(\sigma\), the constructed set has type \(\sigma \toS \boolS\).
A set-valued variable may instead retain the option-function representation selected for its binding; the cast layer below converts it to a graph predicate when a set operator requires one.

The two base-type sets require no recursive call.
\begin{encrule}[base sets]{rule:beer-base-sets}
\[
  \ZintB \encto (\lamS \var{z} : \intS \cdot \mathsf{true},\; \intS \toS \boolS)\mkern20mu
  \ZboolB \encto (\lamS \var{z} : \boolS \cdot \mathsf{true},\; \boolS \toS \boolS)
\]
\end{encrule}
Each becomes the constant-true characteristic predicate over the corresponding SMT base type.

Suppose \(S \encto (S', \sigma \toS \boolS)\).
\begin{encrule}[powerset]{rule:beer-powerset}
\[
  \powB(S) \encto \Bigl(\lamS \var{E} : \sigma \toS \boolS \cdot\; \forallS \var{x} : \sigma \cdot \appS{\var{E}}{\var{x}} \;\impS\; \appS{S'}{\var{x}},\;\; (\sigma \toS \boolS) \toS \boolS\Bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The result recognizes exactly the characteristic predicates included pointwise in \(S'\).

Suppose \(A \encto (A', \sigma_1 \toS \boolS)\) and \(B \encto (B', \sigma_2 \toS \boolS)\).
\begin{encrule}[Cartesian product]{rule:beer-cartesian-product}
\[
  A \prodB B \encto
  \Bigl(\lamS \var{p} : \sigma_1 \timesS \sigma_2 \cdot\;
    \appS{A'}{(\fstS \var{p})} \andS \appS{B'}{(\sndS \var{p})},\;
    (\sigma_1 \timesS \sigma_2) \toS \boolS\Bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The projection form makes explicit that membership in the product is checked componentwise.

Under the same recursive encodings of \(A\) and \(B\), the partial-function space is encoded as follows.
\begin{encrule}[partial-function space]{rule:beer-partial-function-space}
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    A \pfunB B \encto \Bigl(\lamS \var{R} \cdot\;&
      \bigl(\forallS \var{x}, \var{y} \cdot
        \appS{\var{R}}{(\pairS{\var{x}}{\var{y}})}
        \;\impS\; \appS{A'}{\var{x}} \andS \appS{B'}{\var{y}}\bigr)
      \\
      &{}\andS \bigl(\forallS \var{x}, \var{y}, \var{y'} \cdot
        \appS{\var{R}}{(\pairS{\var{x}}{\var{y}})}
        \andS \appS{\var{R}}{(\pairS{\var{x}}{\var{y'}})}
        \;\impS\; \var{y} \eqS \var{y'}\bigr),
      \\
      &((\sigma_1 \timesS \sigma_2) \toS \boolS) \toS \boolS\Bigr).
  \end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
Here \(\var{R} : (\sigma_1 \timesS \sigma_2) \toS \boolS\); the two conjuncts state that its graph lies in \(A \prodB B\) and that it is functional.
All richer B function spaces (\(\tfunB\), \(\injpfunB\), \dots) are comprehensions over \(\pfunB\) (\Cref{ch:b}) and therefore reach the encoder in already-elaborated form.

\subsection{Comprehension and lambda}
\label{sec:beer-rules-comprehension}

Suppose \(D \encto (D', \sigma \toS \boolS)\), and encode \(P \encto (P', \boolS)\) in the context extended with the bound variables \(\vec{\var{v}}\).
Write \(\mathsf{destr}_i(\var{z})\) for the \(i\)-th projection of \(\var{z}\), obtained by a chain of \(\fstS\)/\(\sndS\) applications.
\begin{encrule}[comprehension]{rule:beer-comprehension}
\[
  \bcomp{\vec{\var{v}} \inB D}{P} \encto \Bigl(\lamS \var{z} : \sigma \cdot\; \iteS\bigl(\appS{D'}{\var{z}},\; P'[\vec{\var{v}} \leftarrow \mathsf{destr}(\var{z})],\; \mathsf{false}\bigr),\;\; \sigma \toS \boolS\Bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The guard confines the predicate to \(D'\): outside it, the body---which may be meaningless there---is never consulted, and the predicate returns \(\mathsf{false}\).
This guard is what reconciles the partiality of the B construct with the totality that SMT lambdas demand; the point recurs in the soundness proof (\Cref{sec:beer-case-comprehension}).

Suppose \(D \encto (D', \sigma \toS \boolS)\), and encode \(P \encto (P', \gamma)\) in the context extended with \(\vec{\var{v}}\).
\begin{encrule}[lambda]{rule:beer-lambda}
\[
  \lambdaB \vec{\var{v}} \inB D \cdot P \encto \Bigl(\lamS \var{u} : \sigma \timesS \gamma \cdot\; \appS{D'}{(\fstS \var{u})} \andS \bigl(\sndS \var{u} \eqS P'[\vec{\var{v}} \leftarrow \mathsf{destr}(\fstS \var{u})]\bigr),\;\; (\sigma \timesS \gamma) \toS \boolS\Bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The result is the characteristic predicate of \(\{(x, y) \mid x \in D \wedge y = P(x)\}\), hence the graph of the partial function denoted by the B lambda.
This matches the SMT encoding of the B type: a B lambda has type \(\setB(\tau \prodB \beta)\), a set of pairs, not a function type.
Encoding the graph rather than a function value also spares the encoder any commitment about the lambda's value outside \(D\), where B leaves it undefined.

\subsection{The cast layer}
\label{sec:beer-rules-cast}

An encoded B term of type \(\alpha\) returns its actual representation \(\sigma\) in
\(\bigl[\lgf{(\toSMT{\alpha})},\mgf{(\toSMT{\alpha})}\bigr]\), together with a cast path from \(\sigma\) to \(\toSMT{\alpha}\).
Representation selection is a property of variable bindings: it is decided when a variable is entered in the SMT context, and its term occurrences read the representation recorded there.
When a variable of B type \(\setB(\alpha \prodB \beta)\) is known to range over partial functions, it may be bound at the SMT type
\[
  \toSMT{\alpha} \toS \optS\,\toSMT{\beta}
  \qquad\text{rather than}\qquad
  (\toSMT{\alpha} \timesS \toSMT{\beta}) \toS \boolS,
\]
a partial-function representation that solvers exploit far better than a bare graph predicate.
Since \(\toSMT{\alpha}\) is the upper endpoint of this interval, \(\castable{\sigma}{\toSMT{\alpha}}\); an operation combining differently represented operands first brings them to a common type.
This is the task of the cast combinators.
Each follows the same scheme: compare the operand types with the decidable order \(\sqsubseteq\); if they differ, loosen the tighter operand along the cast path to the more general type (their join), obtaining a fresh variable \(\lvar{x}\) constrained by its specification \(\spec{\lvar{x}}\) to denote the semantic cast image of \(x\) (\Cref{thm:loosening-correctness}); then combine pointwise at the common type.
Incomparable types are rejected (\(\throw\)).

\paragraph{Union and intersection.}
Suppose \(S \encto (S', \gamma \toS \boolS)\) and \(T \encto (T', \gamma \toS \boolS)\).
\begin{encrule}[union and intersection]{rule:beer-union-intersection}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  S \cupB T &\encto
    \bigl(\lamS \var{z} : \gamma \cdot\; \appS{S'}{\var{z}} \orS \appS{T'}{\var{z}},\; \gamma \toS \boolS\bigr) \\
  S \capB T &\encto
    \bigl(\lamS \var{z} : \gamma \cdot\; \appS{S'}{\var{z}} \andS \appS{T'}{\var{z}},\; \gamma \toS \boolS\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
At a common characteristic-predicate type, union and intersection are simply pointwise disjunction and conjunction ({\small\leancodeptr{castUnion}{Encoder/Loosening/Loosening.html\#castUnion}}).

Now suppose \(S \encto (S', \alpha \toS \optS\beta)\) and
\(T \encto (T', (\alpha' \timesS \beta') \toS \boolS)\), with cast paths
\(\castpath{\alpha}{\alpha'}\) and \(\castpath{\beta}{\beta'}\).
Let \(\lvar{S} : (\alpha' \timesS \beta') \toS \boolS\) be obtained by loosening \(S'\) along the induced \(\mathsf{graph}\) path, together with its specification \(\spec{\lvar{S}}\).
\begin{encrule}[union---graph case]{rule:beer-union-graph}
\[
  S \cupB T \encto
  \bigl(\lamS \var{p} : \alpha' \timesS \beta' \cdot\; \appS{\lvar{S}}{\var{p}} \orS \appS{T'}{\var{p}},\;\; (\alpha' \timesS \beta') \toS \boolS\bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The rule declares \(\lvar{S}\) under \(\spec{\lvar{S}}\), thereby turning the functional operand into a graph before taking the pointwise union.
Two further paths (\(\mathsf{fun}\), when both operands are functional, and \(\mathsf{chpred}\)) follow the same pattern at their respective common types; paths that cannot arise from B set operations (\(\mathsf{refl}\) on base types, \(\mathsf{opt}\), \(\mathsf{pair}\)) are rejected.

\paragraph{Equality.}
Suppose \(A \encto (A', \sigma_A)\) and \(B \encto (B', \sigma_B)\).
When \(\castable{\sigma_A}{\sigma_B}\), let \(\lvar{A}\) and \(\spec{\lvar{A}}\) be obtained by loosening \(A'\) to \(\sigma_B\); define \(\lvar{B}\) symmetrically when \(\castable{\sigma_B}{\sigma_A}\).
\begin{encrule}[equality]{rule:beer-equality}
\[
  A \eqB B \encto
  \begin{cases}
    (A' \eqS B',\, \boolS)
      & \text{if } \sigma_A = \sigma_B, \\[2pt]
    ((\lvar{A} \eqS B') \andS \spec{\lvar{A}},\, \boolS)
      & \text{if } \castable{\sigma_A}{\sigma_B},\; \sigma_A \neq \sigma_B, \\[2pt]
    ((\lvar{B} \eqS A') \andS \spec{\lvar{B}},\, \boolS)
      & \text{if } \castable{\sigma_B}{\sigma_A}.
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{encrule}
Equal representations are compared directly; otherwise the tighter operand is loosened to the type of the other ({\small\leancodeptr{castEq}{Encoder/Loosening/Loosening.html\#castEq}}).
Here the specification travels \emph{inside} the returned formula rather than as a global assertion---a scoping necessity, since equalities may occur under binders.

\paragraph{Membership.}
Suppose \(x \encto (x', \sigma)\) and \(S \encto (S', \sigma' \toS \boolS)\).
When required by the type comparison, let \(\lvar{x}\) be the loosening of \(x'\) to \(\sigma'\), or let \(\lvar{S}\) be the loosening of \(S'\) along \(\mathsf{chpred}\) to \(\sigma \toS \boolS\).
\begin{encrule}[membership---characteristic predicate]{rule:beer-membership-chpred}
\[
  x \inB S \encto
  \begin{cases}
    (\appS{S'}{x'},\, \boolS) & \text{if } \sigma = \sigma', \\[2pt]
    (\spec{\lvar{x}} \andS \appS{S'}{\lvar{x}},\, \boolS) & \text{if } \castable{\sigma}{\sigma'},\; \sigma \neq \sigma', \\[2pt]
    (\spec{\lvar{S}} \andS \appS{\lvar{S}}{x'},\, \boolS) & \text{if } \castable{\sigma'}{\sigma}.
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{encrule}
Membership is predicate application after loosening either the element or the predicate to a common domain ({\small\leancodeptr{castMembership}{Encoder/Loosening/Loosening.html\#castMembership}}).

Suppose instead that \(x \encto (x', \sigma_1 \timesS \sigma_2)\) and
\(S \encto (S', \sigma'_1 \toS \optS\sigma'_2)\), with
\(\castable{\sigma_1}{\sigma'_1}\) and \(\castable{\sigma_2}{\sigma'_2}\).
Let \(\lvar{x}\) and \(\spec{\lvar{x}}\) be obtained by loosening \(x'\) componentwise to \(\sigma'_1 \timesS \sigma'_2\).
\begin{encrule}[membership---function]{rule:beer-membership-function}
\[
  x \inB S \encto \bigl(\spec{\lvar{x}} \andS (\appS{S'}{(\fstS \lvar{x})} \eqS \someS (\sndS \lvar{x})),\;\boolS\bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
For a functional representation, membership states that the first component is mapped to the second.
The three remaining subcases dispatch symmetrically, loosening \(\fstS x\), \(\sndS x\), or \(S\) itself componentwise.

\paragraph{Application.}
B application \(\appB f\ x\) must produce a \emph{value}, not a predicate, so a graph representation must be cast in the opposite, tightening direction---the one place where this is sound, because B's well-definedness (\Cref{sec:b-wd}) guarantees \(x \in \domB(f)\) and functionality of \(f\).
Suppose \(f \encto (f', (\tau \timesS \sigma) \toS \boolS)\) and \(x \encto (x', \xi)\), with \(\castable{\tau}{\xi}\).
Let \(\lvar{f} : (\xi \timesS \sigma) \toS \boolS\) be obtained by loosening \(f'\), and introduce a fresh function witness \(\var{f}^{!!} : \xi \toS \optS\sigma\).
\begin{encrule}[application---graph]{rule:beer-application-graph}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \spec{{\var{f}^{!!}}} &\;\triangleq\;
    \forallS \var{u} : \xi,\, \var{v} : \sigma \cdot\;
    \appS{\lvar{f}}{\pairS{\var{u}}{\var{v}}}
      \eqS \bigl(\appS{\var{f}^{!!}}{\var{u}} \eqS \someS \var{v}\bigr), \\
  \appB f\ x &\;\encto\;
    \bigl(\theS (\appS{\var{f}^{!!}}{x'}),\; \sigma\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{encrule}
The specification pins \(\var{f}^{!!}\) to the graph \(\lvar{f}\); application then extracts the defined option payload with \(\theS\) ({\small\leancodeptr{castApp}{Encoder/Loosening/Loosening.html\#castApp}}).

If \(f \encto (f', \tau \toS \optS\sigma)\) and \(x \encto (x', \tau)\), no function witness is required.
\begin{encrule}[application---function]{rule:beer-application-function}
\[
  \appB f\ x \encto \bigl(\theS(\appS{f'}{x'}),\; \sigma\bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The functional representation can be applied directly; when the domain types merely compare under \(\sqsubseteq\), the encoder first loosens \(f'\) or \(x'\) and then uses the same rule.

\subsection{Binders and the universal quantifier}
\label{sec:beer-rules-quantifier}

The last rule is the bounded universal quantifier, whose translation must solve a scoping problem that no other rule has.
Encoding the body \(P\) and the domain guard may itself invoke the cast layer, producing fresh helper variables (\(\lvar{x}\), \(\var{f}^{!!}\), \dots) together with their specifications.
Helpers generated for the \emph{bound} variables \(\vec{\var{v}}\) cannot be declared globally: their values depend on the quantified variables and would otherwise escape their scope.
The encoder therefore re-scopes them \emph{inside} the quantifier, universally bound and guarded by their specifications.
Suppose \(D \encto (D', \sigma \toS \boolS)\).
Replace the bound variables \(\vec{\var{v}}\) by fresh binders \(\vec{\var{z}}\) of types \(\vec{\tau}\), then encode the membership guard and \(P \encto (P', \boolS)\) in the extended context, collecting the generated helpers \(\var{h}_1, \dots, \var{h}_k\).
\begin{encrule}[bounded universal]{rule:beer-bounded-universal}
\[
  \forallB \vec{\var{v}} \inB D \cdot P
  \;\encto\;
  \Bigl(\forallS \vec{\var{z}} : \vec{\tau} \cdot\;
    \forallS \var{h}_1 \cdots \forallS \var{h}_k \cdot\;
    \spec{{\var{h}_1}} \impS \cdots \impS \spec{{\var{h}_k}} \impS
    \bigl( (\vec{\var{z}} \inB D)' \impS P' \bigr),\;\;
  \boolS\Bigr)
\]
\end{encrule}
The helper binders keep every generated specification in the scope of the variables on which it depends.
Here \((\vec{\var{z}} \inB D)'\) abbreviates the membership encoding of \Cref{sec:beer-rules-cast}.
The binder types \(\vec{\tau}\) are derived from the component types of \(\sigma\); a component is changed to an option-function type only when that bound name is explicitly selected for a functional representation.
Semantic agreement already ensures that every value in the B domain has a representative at the element type carried by the encoded domain, recursively at nested set positions.
If the bound names receive a different tuple type, the general term theorem additionally requires those source values to have preimages at that type through the corresponding semantic cast and canonical retraction.
When no independent functional representation is selected for a bound name, the support already carried by the represented domain discharges this condition; an option-function selection instead requires semantic functionhood of the domain values.
The guarded-universal shape \(\forallS \var{h} \cdot \spec{\var{h}} \impS \cdots\)---rather than the seemingly natural \(\existsS \var{h} \cdot \spec{\var{h}} \andS \cdots\)---is a soundness matter, not a stylistic one: the argument, which hinges on the fact that specifications determine their helpers uniquely, is given with the soundness proof of this case (\Cref{sec:beer-case-forall}).

On the running example, the goal \(\var{f} \cupB \var{g} \subseteqB X \prodB Y\) is elaborated to \(\forallB \var{x} \inB \var{f} \cupB \var{g} \cdot \var{x} \inB X \prodB Y\), whose encoding instantiates the \encruleref{rule:beer-bounded-universal} rule.
The domain encoding triggers the \encruleref{rule:beer-union-graph} rule: the functional \(\var{f}\) is loosened to a graph predicate \(\lvar{f}\), which becomes a helper universally bound under its graph specification; the resulting pointwise union is the guard, and membership in \(X \prodB Y\) supplies the conclusion.
The complete emitted script is shown in \Cref{sec:beer-proof-obligations}.
