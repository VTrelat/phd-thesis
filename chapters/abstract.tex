\noindent
The B method reduces the correctness of software development to the
verification of proof obligations, so its guarantees rest on the tools that
discharge these obligations and on the translations that carry them to those
tools. This thesis follows B proof obligations along two routes, automated and
interactive, and establishes for each what a discharged obligation means. It
contributes three Lean~4 developments. \zflean{} is a library for set-level
mathematics in Mathlib's model of ZFC: a relational calculus with automation,
canonical constructions of numbers, relations, and functions, and bridges to
Lean's native types. In its universe, fragments of B and of SMT-LIB~2.7
receive their syntax, typing, and denotational semantics, so that the
correctness of a translation becomes a statement about denotations. \beer{},
built on this setting, is a verified encoder of Atelier~B proof obligations
into SMT-LIB. Because B objects are sets while SMT-LIB terms carry fixed
sorts, one B value admits several target representations, a set becoming a
characteristic predicate and a functional relation a function; type loosening
orders these representations and moves terms between them without changing
the denoted set. The mechanized soundness theorem states that every successful
encoding of a well-typed, well-defined term agrees semantically with its
source at the selected representation; in particular, for proof obligations,
truth is preserved. On nearly a million goals from industrial B developments,
compared with Atelier~B's ppTrans under the SMT solver cvc5, the encoding of
\beer{} proves a smaller share end to end, mostly for lack of coverage; on the
goals both pipelines answer it is within a few points, and each proves goals
the other does not. \barrel{}, developed with Ghilain Bergeron, imports
Atelier~B obligations as Lean theorems. It makes the well-definedness of
partial operators a typing constraint, so that no term is built without
evidence of its definedness, closes routine goals automatically, and leaves
the rest to interactive proof in Lean. Each development states what it proves
and what it trusts, so that increasingly powerful automation, AI-assisted
included, can be used without making the validity of a result depend on the
tool that proposed it.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} B method; higher-order logic; SMT solving; verified
encoding; set theory; ZFC; interactive theorem proving; automated reasoning; Lean~4.
