\section{Manuscript overview and conventions}
\label{sec:manuscript-overview}

This section outlines the structure of the manuscript and the conventions used
throughout.

\subsection{Organization}
The manuscript proceeds from common foundations to the automated and
interactive reasoning routes introduced in \Cref{subsec:intro-itp-atp}.
\Cref{ch:background} introduces the mathematical and technical material used
throughout the thesis: axiomatic set theory, the B method, SMT solving, Lean,
and the representation of syntax with binders. \Cref{ch:zflean} then develops
\zflean, the set-theoretic library that provides the common semantic universe
for the subsequent formalizations. The next two chapters define the languages
on either side of the verified translation. \Cref{ch:b} formalizes the relevant
fragment of the B mathematical language, while \Cref{ch:smt} gives a parallel
account of the target fragment of SMT-LIB~2.7; both chapters cover syntax,
typing, and denotational semantics.

The following chapters build the reasoning tools themselves.
\Cref{ch:loosening} introduces type loosening, which reconciles the different
representations that a single set-theoretic value may require in the typed
target language. \Cref{ch:beer} uses these foundations to present \beer, the
verified automated encoding of B proof obligations into SMT-LIB.
\Cref{ch:evaluation} assesses that encoding on an industrial corpus of proof
obligations. \Cref{ch:barrel} then develops the complementary interactive route
through \barrel, which brings B proof obligations into Lean. The concluding chapter summarizes the results,
their limitations, and the directions they open.

\subsection{Mathematical presentation}
This manuscript deliberately adopts a mathematical register: it presents the
encodings and their metatheory through definitions, typing judgments, and
denotations rather than by reproducing the Lean code that realizes them. The
artifact behind the thesis is nonetheless an executable, formally verified
program, and some implementation concerns are inseparable from its correctness
argument. Chief among these is the \emph{monadic} organization of the encoder:
it threads a typing context, allocates fresh names, and accumulates the emitted
SMT script. We make this organization explicit where it bears on a design
decision or a proof, chiefly in
\Cref{sec:lean-implementation-architecture,sec:binders-names-contexts}, and keep
the state it manipulates implicit elsewhere.

\subsection{Color conventions}
Color provides a consistent visual guide to the kind of material being read.
The manuscript uses the following conventions throughout:

\smallskip
\begin{description}[
    style=multiline,
    nosep,
    leftmargin=30mm,
    labelwidth=18mm,
    labelsep=5mm,
    align=left
  ]
  \item[\overviewcolorbox{pink}{white}{Pink}]
    Theorems, lemmas, and corollaries.
  \item[\overviewcolorbox{orange}{white}{Orange}]
    Definitions of mathematical objects, relations, and judgments.
  \item[\overviewcolorbox{green}{white}{Green}]
    Examples of definitions and constructions.
  \item[\overviewcolorbox{blue}{dark-blue}{Blue}]
    Remarks, facts, proofs, and internal cross-references.
  \item[\overviewcolorbox{beige}{cover-cream}{Beige}]
    Notes, encoding rules, and source-code listings.
\end{description}

Internal cross-references, citations, and external web addresses are clickable.
Definition and theorems generally have a direct counterpart in the formal
Lean artifact, indicated in their upper-right corner as lists one or more Lean declaration
names pointing to the generated Lean documentation at the corresponding
declaration, and allowing the reader to inspect the machine-checked statement and
its implementation. The same convention is occasionally used for declaration
names in running text. These links provide a route into the artifact, thereby
increasing trust in the informal content of the manuscript, allowing the reader
to check every single result it claims.

\subsection{Notations}
\paragraph{Superscripts.}
The notation distinguishes systematically between the languages and levels of
representation used in the thesis. A superscript \(B\) marks concrete B-related
constructs like \(\bop{\vdash}\) and \(\mapletB\),
while \(B'\) marks their abstract counterparts \(\baop{\vdash}\) and \(\baop{\mapsto}\);
similarly, superscript \(S\) and \(S'\) mark concrete and abstract SMT-related constructs, \eg
\(\sop{\vdash}\) and \(\lamS\), and their abstract counterparts \(\saop{\vdash}\) and \(\saop{\lambda}\).
A superscript \(Z\) identifies
operations or values in the shared ZFC semantic universe, \eg \(\addZ\) and \(\lamZ\).
These superscripts are category tags, not exponents, and serve as visual
disambiguation helpers. Object-language variables are typeset in bold gray,
as in \(\var{x}\); other specialized conventions are introduced at their first use.

\paragraph{Definitional equality.}
We write \(\regnotation{\coloneq}\) for \emph{definitional assignment}---for
example, when binding a name to a term or giving the body of a definition. This
is distinct both from propositional equality \(=\) and from the metatheoretic
symbol \(\regnotation{\triangleq}\), read ``is defined as,'' which is used in
displayed mathematics. When a proof component is unused or irrelevant in a
given context, we sometimes replace it by ``\(\regnotation{\irrelprf}\),'' following
Lean notation.

\paragraph{List of all notations.}
The first defining occurrence of each registered symbol is recorded in the
\nameref{ch:list-of-notations} (p.~\pageref{ch:list-of-notations}). Entries are grouped by chapter and retain
their order of introduction; each page number links back to the point at which
the symbol is first defined.
