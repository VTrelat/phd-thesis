The distinction between automated and interactive reasoning becomes concrete in
this thesis through the B method, a formal method for specifying and refining
systems~\cite{abrial1996bbook}. A B development generates logical claims, called
\emph{proof obligations}, that must be discharged to establish the desired
correctness properties. Large developments may generate many such obligations:
routine ones favor automated discharge, while the remaining obligations require
inspection and guidance. This organization is summarized in
\Cref{fig:intro-reasoning-routes}. This thesis addresses these two needs through
three open-source developments in Lean~4.

\subsection{A set-theoretic foundation}
\label{subsec:intro-contrib-zflean}

\zflean is a Lean~4 library for carrying out ordinary
mathematics inside Mathlib's model of ZFC set theory. Although Mathlib constructs
such a model, using it directly exposes quotient and extensionality details that
make even familiar arguments cumbersome. \zflean provides a relational calculus
with targeted automation, canonical constructions for Booleans, numbers,
relations, and functions, and bridges between ZFC objects and Lean's native
types. These bridges allow proofs to move between extensional set-level
reasoning and typed results from Mathlib without conflating the two foundations.

Within this thesis, \zflean has a semantic role. It allows B and SMT-LIB
to be interpreted in the same ZFC universe, so that correctness of the encoding
can be expressed as a precise set-theoretic correspondence between denotations,
rather than as an informal comparison of unrelated models. \Cref{ch:zflean}
presents the library, its relational calculus, its mathematical constructions,
and its interoperability layer.

\subsection{Verified automated reasoning}
\label{subsec:intro-contrib-beer}

The second contribution, \beer (B Encoder), follows the automated route. It
encodes Atelier~B proof obligations to SMT-LIB~2.7, and the translation is
verified in Lean. The development formalizes the relevant fragments of B
and SMT-LIB, together with their typing and denotational semantics, and
interprets both fragments in the set-theoretic universe provided by \zflean.

The main technical difficulty is that a single B value may admit several useful
SMT representations. A B set may become a characteristic predicate, while a
relation known to be functional may be represented by an SMT function. This
thesis introduces \emph{representation loosening} to describe when and how a
value can move between such representations, and integrates it into the
mechanized soundness proof of the encoder. The resulting theorem relates the
denotation of each supported, well-typed, and well-defined source term to that of
the SMT term produced by every successful encoding; for Boolean proof
obligations, it yields preservation of truth. The languages, representation
theory, executable encoder, and soundness proof are developed in
\Cref{ch:b,ch:smt,ch:loosening,ch:beer}.

This result verifies the translation independently of the search performed by
the SMT solver. Trust in a solver answer is a separate concern: the answer may
be accepted directly or justified by checking a proof certificate. Separating
these obligations makes clear which part of the automated route is established
by this thesis.

\begin{quote}\small
  \fullcite{trelat2025beer}
\end{quote}

Maintaining the encoder and its soundness proof in lockstep necessarily limits
how quickly its supported fragment can evolve. For experiments that prioritize
coverage and translation speed, \textsc{Zero-Proof} \beer{} deliberately omits
the mechanized correctness argument. It is therefore shown in
\Cref{fig:intro-reasoning-routes} as the broader-coverage variant, but it does
not carry the verified encoder's guarantee.

\subsection{Bringing B proof obligations to Lean}
\label{subsec:intro-contrib-barrel}

The third contribution, \barrel (B Automated tRanslation for Reasoning in Lean),
follows the interactive route and was developed jointly with Ghilain Bergeron.
It connects the existing Atelier~B workflow to Lean by reading generated proof
obligations, preserving their structure and familiar notation where possible,
and presenting them as ordinary Lean theorem statements. Users may then combine
domain-specific lemmas with Lean tactics and Mathlib, while Lean's kernel checks
every completed proof.

A central design problem is the treatment of B's partial operators. Their Lean
encodings require explicit evidence of well-definedness. \barrel turns this
evidence into additional goals before exposing the main proof obligation, so
ill-typed or ill-defined instantiations are rejected by construction rather
than left to prover discipline. Equal well-definedness goals are shared across
an import, and proof-producing automation combines B-specific lemmas with
Lean's \texttt{grind} tactic before leaving the remaining invariant and
refinement arguments for interactive proof. Obligation-local resource limits
and a live infoview panel make this workflow usable on large proof-obligation
files. \Cref{ch:barrel} presents the architecture, embedding, metaprogramming
interface, and representative developments.

The ``kernel-checked'' tag in \Cref{fig:intro-reasoning-routes} states the precise
scope of the guarantee. Lean checks the proof of each generated theorem, while
\barrel currently includes Atelier~B's generation of the original obligation
and the faithfulness of its B-to-Lean shallow embedding in its trusted base.

Together, these contributions instantiate the architecture developed in
\Cref{sec:intro-motivation}: \zflean supplies the semantic setting, \beer
establishes a verified bridge to automated reasoning, and \barrel provides an
interactive route whose completed proofs are kernel-checked. Their guarantees
are deliberately not collapsed into a single claim of ``trustworthiness'';
each development identifies what is checked and what remains trusted. The next
section situates this work with respect to prior approaches to proof obligations,
automated reasoning, B encodings, proof certificates, and verified translation.
