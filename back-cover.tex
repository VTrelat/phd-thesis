      Turn this book over and watch two foundationally distinct worlds flowing into one another. On one side, the fluid world of sets, where everything is a collection and a function is just a well-behaved relation; on the other, the crystalline world of types, where every object carries its shape. Formal specification lives mostly on the first side: whether they describe systems or mathematics, formalisms from B, Z and TLA$^{+}$ to Mizar and Isabelle/ZF speak of sets, relations and functions, and state correctness as facts about them. The provers that establish these facts live on the second, among fixed types and sorted signatures. Somewhere in between, a translation decides what the prover is actually asked. That translation is part of the proof, and it is almost never proved.
      \smallskip

      This thesis takes up that proof for the B method, and builds the tools that make it worthwhile. \zflean turns Mathlib's model of ZFC into a workspace where set-theoretic mathematics is practical, and where B and SMT-LIB can be interpreted side by side. \beer, built on it, encodes Atelier B proof obligations into SMT-LIB with a formal proof that nothing is lost on the way: type loosening lets a value flow between its representations, from function to relation, without changing what it denotes, and the soundness theorem ensures that every successful encoding preserves meaning, hence truth. \barrel opens another door, where obligations become Lean theorems whose partial operators are well-defined by construction, a typing constraint that no proof can bypass.
      \smallskip

      On nearly a million industrial goals, the encoding holds its own against Atelier B's own translator. And the two routes complete each other: what one automates, the other leaves to be finished by hand. On both, it is always clear where proof ends and trust begins.\par}
